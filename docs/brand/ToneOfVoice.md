# Tone of Voice

**Roman Creative Studio Brand Bible — Vol. 1, Part A**
*Version 1.0 | Last Updated: June 2026*

---

## Purpose of This Document

Brand voice is consistent (see BrandVoice.md). Tone adjusts. The same personality — confident, clear, strategic, warm — expresses itself differently depending on context, audience, and the emotional state of the reader. This document defines when and how to shift tone.

---

## Voice vs. Tone

**Voice:** Who we are. Constant. The underlying personality.

**Tone:** How we express that personality in a specific moment. Variable. Responsive to context.

A confident, warm person speaks differently at a business dinner than at a close friend's celebration. The personality doesn't change — the expression of it does. That's tone.

## Tone Variations

---

### 1. Professional

**When to use:**
- Proposals and contracts
- Client introductions
- Formal project updates
- Any first-touch communication with a new prospect

**Characteristics:**
- Precise language, no contractions where formality is warranted
- Clear structure: purpose stated first, details follow
- No jokes or casual observations
- Confident recommendations, not hedged suggestions

**Example:**
> *Thank you for the introduction. I've reviewed your current website and have some initial thoughts I'd like to share. I've attached a brief overview of how we typically approach projects like yours. I'm available for a call this week if you'd like to connect.*

**When not to use:**
- Ongoing client relationships where warmth has been established
- Social media
- Educational content where approachability aids comprehension

---

### 2. Friendly

**When to use:**
- Established client relationships
- Social media (especially Instagram, LinkedIn personal posts)
- Post-launch communications
- Thank-you notes and milestone acknowledgments

**Characteristics:**
- Contractions are natural
- Genuine warmth — not performed or hollow
- Personal observations are appropriate
- Still clear and direct — friendly doesn't mean vague

**Example:**
> *Your new site is live and it looks great. Really glad we got to work on this together. Let me know how the first few weeks go — I'd love to hear how people respond.*

**When not to use:**
- First contact with a prospect (too casual too soon)
- Difficult conversations (scope disputes, late payments)
- Legal or contract documents

---

### 3. Educational

**When to use:**
- Blog posts and articles
- Resource guides (lead magnets)
- Social media educational content
- FAQs and help documentation
- Any situation where we are explaining something the reader doesn't yet know

**Characteristics:**
- Assume the reader is intelligent but not an expert
- Break complex ideas into clear steps or sections
- Use specific examples, not vague generalities
- Generous with knowledge — teach without gatekeeping
- Active voice, present tense where possible

**Example:**
> *Most business owners think SEO is about keywords. It's not — or not primarily. It's about giving Google the clearest possible signal that your page is the best answer to a specific question. Here's how that actually works.*

**When not to use:**
- Sales conversations (don't lecture a prospect; ask them questions)
- Design presentations (show the thinking; don't walk through a course)

---

### 4. Encouraging

**When to use:**
- Onboarding and kickoff communications
- Mid-project check-ins
- Client feedback sessions (especially when the client is nervous)
- Post-launch follow-ups

**Characteristics:**
- Acknowledges where the client is and validates the effort
- Forward-looking — focuses on what's possible
- Genuine, not cheerleader-performative
- Pairs encouragement with substance: *"This is looking great, and here's what we do next to make it even stronger."*

**Example:**
> *The feedback on this design round has been really useful. You've identified exactly the right things. The next round will be tighter because of it.*

**When not to use:**
- Situations where the client needs a clear, direct message (encouragement can soften a message that shouldn't be softened)
- Technical QA documentation or checklists

---

### 5. Technical

**When to use:**
- Developer documentation
- Internal AOS documentation
- Technical specifications in proposals
- Code comments

**Characteristics:**
- Precision over warmth
- Specific values, measurements, and implementation details
- Assume technical literacy in the reader
- No softening of technical requirements with casual language

**Example:**
> *All images must include `width` and `height` attributes to prevent layout shift (CLS). Use WebP format with a JPG fallback. Target file size: under 150 KB for hero images, under 80 KB for card images.*

**When not to use:**
- Any client-facing communication (translate technical requirements into plain language)
- Marketing and sales content

---

### 6. Sales

**When to use:**
- Discovery calls
- Proposal presentations
- Follow-up communications with warm leads
- Landing pages with direct conversion goals

**Characteristics:**
- Leads with the client's problem, not our services
- Outcome-focused: what changes for them if they work with us
- Confident without pressure — we never push or create false urgency
- Specific: *"Businesses like yours typically see X after Y"* not vague benefits

**Example:**
> *Based on what you've described, the biggest issue isn't the design — it's that the site doesn't guide visitors to take action. We can fix that. Here's what that would look like for your business.*

**When not to use:**
- Established client communications (they've already said yes)
- Educational content (readers sense the sales motive and disengage)

---

### 7. Support

**When to use:**
- Responding to client issues post-launch
- Care Plan client communications
- Any situation where a client is experiencing a problem

**Characteristics:**
- Acknowledge the issue immediately — no defensiveness
- State what is being done about it
- Set a clear timeline for resolution
- Follow up when resolved, unprompted

**Example:**
> *I see the issue you're describing — thank you for flagging it. I'm looking at it now and will have it resolved within the hour. I'll follow up as soon as it's fixed.*

**When not to use:**
- Standard project communication (support mode is for problems, not normal workflow)

---

## Tone Dos and Don'ts

| Always | Never |
|--------|-------|
| Match tone to the emotional context of the reader | Use the same tone for every situation |
| Transition tone gradually as relationships develop | Jump to casual familiarity in first contact |
| Stay warm even when being professional | Be cold in the name of professionalism |
| Be honest even when the tone is encouraging | Use encouraging tone to avoid hard truths |
| Use technical tone internally | Use technical tone in client-facing copy |
