# Tone of Voice

## The Distinction: Voice vs. Tone

**Voice** is who we are — consistent across all content (see BrandVoice.md).  
**Tone** is how we express that voice in a specific context — it adjusts based on the situation, audience, and emotional context.

---

## Tone Map by Situation

### Homepage / Hero Section
**Tone:** Bold, clear, aspirational  
We're establishing credibility and creating desire in the first 5 seconds. Confident statements, strong verbs, outcome-focused language.

> *"We build digital experiences that grow businesses."*

### Service Pages
**Tone:** Educational, specific, trust-building  
We're explaining what we do and why it matters to this specific type of client. More detail, more empathy for their specific situation.

> *"Dental patients judge your practice by your website before they ever walk in the door. We make sure that first impression works in your favor."*

### About Page
**Tone:** Personal, purposeful, honest  
We share who we are, why we do this work, and what we stand for. More story, more humanity. Still professional.

### Pricing Page
**Tone:** Direct, transparent, reassuring  
No games. Clear prices, clear scope, clear rationale. Anticipate hesitation and address it calmly.

> *"These aren't the cheapest prices you'll find. They reflect what it takes to do the work properly and deliver results that justify the investment."*

### Blog / Educational Content
**Tone:** Authoritative, generous, practical  
We're giving real value. No thin content, no keyword stuffing. We write like someone who knows the subject and genuinely wants to help.

### Proposals
**Tone:** Professional, specific, solution-focused  
The proposal is a strategic document. Tone is formal but not cold. Every section demonstrates that we understood the client's specific situation.

### Client Communication (Email / Messages)
**Tone:** Warm, direct, action-oriented  
Respect the client's time. Get to the point. Be helpful. End with a clear next step.

### Error Pages / Empty States
**Tone:** Light, helpful, slightly self-aware  
A 404 page is an opportunity to show personality. Keep it brief, redirect helpfully, and don't make the user feel blamed.

## Words We Use

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| Build | Leverage |
| Grow | Scale (overused) |
| Strategy | Synergy |
| Results | ROI-driven solutions |
| Partner | Vendor |
| Invest | Cost |
| Clients | Customers (in most contexts) |
| We | I (agency voice) |
| Digital presence | Online footprint |
| Business goals | KPIs (in client-facing copy) |

## Punctuation and Style Notes

- **Em dashes** (—) over hyphens for parenthetical statements
- **Serial commas** always
- **Sentence case** for headings in most contexts
- **Numbers:** Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10+
- **Avoid exclamation points** except when genuinely celebratory (client wins, launches)
- **Short sentences** for emphasis. Longer sentences for explanation and context.
