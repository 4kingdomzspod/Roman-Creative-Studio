# Competitive Positioning

**Roman Creative Studio Brand Bible — Vol. 1, Part A**
*Version 1.0 | Last Updated: June 2026*

---

## Purpose of This Document

Understanding the competitive landscape is essential for pricing, messaging, and sales. This document defines who we compete with, where we fit, our strengths and weaknesses relative to each competitor type, and how to position Roman Creative Studio in a sales conversation.

---

## The Competitive Landscape

### Tier 1: Enterprise and Large Agencies

**Examples:** National digital agencies, full-service marketing firms, major city design studios

**Price range:** $25,000–$500,000+

**What they offer:**
- Large multidisciplinary teams
- Full-service (strategy, brand, web, ads, PR)
- Established brand recognition
- Deep process and project management

**Their weaknesses:**
- Inaccessible to small businesses financially
- Slow — projects take months, sometimes years
- Multiple layers between the client and the actual work
- Small business clients get junior-level attention

**Our position vs. them:**
We deliver comparable quality at 10–20% of the price for clients who don't need a 20-person team. Direct relationship with the practitioner. Faster. More agile. Just as strategic.

---

### Tier 2: Regional and Mid-Market Agencies

**Examples:** Local agencies with 5–20 employees, regional digital marketing firms

**Price range:** $8,000–$30,000

**What they offer:**
- Professional process
- Multiple service offerings
- Account management
- Established local reputation

**Their weaknesses:**
- Account manager overhead adds cost without adding value
- Often generalist — no deep industry expertise
- Client gets a team member, not the principal
- Recurring services often underdelivered once the project is sold

**Our position vs. them:**
Leaner operation equals lower overhead and better value. The person clients speak with is the person doing the work. We bring deeper industry expertise for specific sectors. Our quality competes at a better price point.

---

### Tier 3: Freelancers and Solo Designers

**Examples:** Upwork designers, local freelancers, "web design" sole proprietors

**Price range:** $500–$8,000

**What they offer:**
- Lower cost
- Fast availability
- Personal relationship

**Their weaknesses:**
- Little to no strategy layer
- Inconsistent quality and process
- No ongoing support structure
- Scalability risk — one person, no backup
- Often execute what the client asks for rather than what the client needs

**Our position vs. them:**
We are not competing with freelancers. Clients who choose us over a freelancer are choosing strategy and a documented process over execution and lower cost. We serve clients who have outgrown the freelancer model — or who tried it and found it insufficient.

---

### Tier 4: DIY Builders

**Examples:** Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy Website Builder, Webflow (self-managed)

**Price range:** $0–$500/year

**What they offer:**
- Complete control
- Low cost
- Speed of setup

**Their weaknesses:**
- No strategy
- Template limitations
- Time investment from the business owner
- Generic results — looks like every other Squarespace site
- No conversion optimization
- SEO limited by platform constraints

**Our position vs. them:**
We serve clients who've tried DIY and found it didn't reflect who they are — or who understand from the start that their time is worth more than the hours it would take to build and maintain a site themselves. The real cost of DIY is not the subscription fee — it's the business lost to a better-presenting competitor.

---

### Tier 5: Template Marketplaces

**Examples:** ThemeForest, TemplateMonster, premium WordPress theme shops

**Price range:** $30–$200 per template

**What they offer:**
- Fast visual starting point
- Low cost
- Professional appearance at a glance

**Their weaknesses:**
- Same template used by thousands of other businesses
- Requires technical setup and customization
- No strategy layer
- No conversion thinking
- Often performance-heavy and accessibility-deficient

**Our position vs. them:**
We start from the client's business, not from a template. The result is a site built for their specific goals, customers, and market — not an adapted starting point with their logo placed over someone else's structure.

## Our Honest Weaknesses

**We are not the fastest option.**
Doing strategy before design takes time. Clients who need something in a week will not find us the right fit.

**We are not the cheapest option.**
By design. Competing on price is not our strategy.

**We cannot yet compete on team depth.**
A large agency can field 10 specialists simultaneously. We cannot — yet. For clients with complex, multi-channel campaigns needing a full team, we refer appropriately.

**Our portfolio is still growing.**
For clients whose primary decision factor is an extensive category-specific portfolio, we are building toward that position.

---

## How to Position in a Sales Conversation

Never name competitors directly. Position against the *type* of experience:

> *"We're not a template shop, and we're not a $100K agency. We're in the middle — the place where you get genuine strategy, premium execution, and a direct relationship with the person doing the work. That's the gap most businesses can't find a good answer to, and it's exactly where we operate."*
