# Core Values

**Roman Creative Studio Brand Bible — Vol. 1, Part A**
*Version 1.0 | Last Updated: June 2026*

---

## Purpose of This Document

Core values are not aspirational statements on a wall. They are operational commitments — the standards that define how Roman Creative Studio works, who it hires, which clients it accepts, and what it is willing to walk away from.

When values feel inconvenient, that is when they matter most.

---

## The Values

---

### 1. Craftsmanship

**What It Means**
Every deliverable — a website, a proposal, a documentation file, an email — is done with care and attention to detail. We don't ship work we're not proud of. We don't cut corners to save time. We don't release something good enough when excellent is achievable.

**Practical Example**
A client needs a website in 3 weeks. The timeline is tight. Rather than reducing quality, we reduce scope — deliver fewer pages at a high standard rather than more pages at a compromised one.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Review work before presenting it — always
- Never send a proposal or client-facing document without proofreading
- Treat every page of every client site with the same care as the homepage
- Hold to quality standards even under deadline pressure

**Decision Making Guidance**
If the work isn't something you'd put in your portfolio, it's not done yet.

---

### 2. Integrity

**What It Means**
We say what we mean. We do what we say. We tell clients the truth — especially when it's uncomfortable. We don't oversell, overpromise, or misrepresent what we can deliver.

**Practical Example**
A client asks if we can launch in two weeks. We can't without compromising quality. We say so directly, explain why, and propose a realistic timeline rather than agreeing and underdelivering.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Never agree to a scope or timeline we don't believe in
- Communicate problems early — not after they've compounded
- Be honest about capability gaps; refer out when needed
- Document agreements in writing

**Decision Making Guidance**
If you wouldn't want this conversation to be read back to the client in six months, don't have it.

---

### 3. Strategic Thinking

**What It Means**
We lead with strategy, not aesthetics. Every recommendation we make is rooted in a business goal, a customer psychology insight, or a market reality. We are advisors who also execute — not executors who also give opinions.

**Practical Example**
A client wants a homepage redesign with 8 sections. We ask what the homepage is supposed to accomplish. It turns out 3 of those sections address goals the client doesn't actually have. We propose 5 sections — each with a defined purpose.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Start every project by defining success — measurable, specific
- Question requests that lack a clear business rationale
- Connect design decisions to business outcomes in every presentation
- Push back on aesthetic-only feedback when it contradicts strategy

**Decision Making Guidance**
If you can't explain why a decision helps the client's business, it may not belong in the project.

---

### 4. Genuine Partnership

**What It Means**
We treat every client's business as if it were our own. We are invested in their outcomes — not just in completing deliverables. The engagement does not end at launch. The relationship continues.

**Practical Example**
A client's Google Business Profile has obvious errors we notice while reviewing their site. We mention it — even though it's not in our scope — because a genuine partner doesn't walk past a problem they can see.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Proactively share insights even when not directly asked
- Follow up after launch to ask about results
- Celebrate client wins — genuinely
- Refer clients to trusted resources when their needs exceed our scope

**Decision Making Guidance**
Ask: would we want this for a business we personally cared about? If no, reconsider.

---

### 5. Continuous Learning

**What It Means**
The digital landscape changes faster than any static knowledge base. We stay current not out of obligation, but because it directly serves our clients. What we knew two years ago is not enough for the work we're doing today.

**Practical Example**
Core Web Vitals become a ranking factor. We study the technical implications, update our development standards, and apply the changes to existing client sites before they ask.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Follow industry publications, design leaders, and SEO developments actively
- Document new learning in the AOS so the whole operation improves
- Challenge existing approaches when better ones are found
- Share knowledge with clients — educate, don't gatekeep

**Decision Making Guidance**
If we're doing something the same way we did it 2 years ago, ask whether that's still the right way.

---

### 6. Long-Term Thinking

**What It Means**
We optimize for outcomes over time, not immediate convenience. We make decisions — about clients, pricing, technology, and partnerships — that serve the company 3 years from now, not just this month.

**Practical Example**
A lead wants a project at a rate below our standard. They have a large network and offer visibility in return. We decline. Discounting our work to gain exposure sets a precedent that devalues everything we do.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Build systems and documentation that compound over time
- Choose recurring revenue over one-time windfalls where appropriate
- Invest in relationships that have long-term potential, not just immediate value
- Make decisions that future-you will be proud of

**Decision Making Guidance**
If this decision feels convenient now but creates a problem in 12 months, it's probably the wrong decision.

---

### 7. Ownership

**What It Means**
We take full responsibility for our work — the outcomes, the process, the mistakes, and the recovery. We don't deflect. We don't blame tools, clients, or circumstances. We find the solution and we own the result.

**Practical Example**
A launched site has a mobile bug that wasn't caught in QA. The immediate response is not to explain why it happened — it's to fix it, communicate clearly with the client, and improve the QA process so it doesn't happen again.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Acknowledge mistakes before the client has to point them out
- Fix problems without being asked twice
- Take initiative — don't wait for permission to do the right thing
- Own the success and the failure equally

**Decision Making Guidance**
Before explaining why something went wrong, ask: what are we doing to fix it right now?

---

### 8. Business Results

**What It Means**
Beauty without function is decoration. Strategy without execution is theory. We are in the business of producing outcomes — increased leads, better conversions, stronger credibility, higher search visibility. Everything we build is measured against this standard.

**Practical Example**
A website redesign launches. 60 days later, we review the analytics with the client. Organic traffic is up 40%. Contact form submissions doubled. We document this, share it with the client, and ask what to work on next.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Define success metrics before the project begins
- Check in on results post-launch
- Build analytics into every project by default
- Report results honestly — including when results need more time

**Decision Making Guidance**
If we can't connect a design or strategy decision to a measurable business outcome, we question whether it belongs.

---

### 9. Accessibility and Inclusion

**What It Means**
Every website we build should be usable by every person — including those who navigate by keyboard, use screen readers, or have visual impairments. Accessibility is not a feature. It is a baseline standard of quality.

**Practical Example**
A client asks to skip the accessibility audit to save time. We explain that accessibility is part of our standard, not an add-on — and that inaccessible websites also rank lower in search and expose the client to legal risk.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Include accessibility review in every project's QA phase
- Never remove `alt` text or aria attributes to save time
- Design for keyboard navigation from the start, not as an afterthought
- Educate clients about why accessibility matters for their business

**Decision Making Guidance**
Would a person using a screen reader have a full, frustration-free experience on this site? If not, it's not done.

---

### 10. Professionalism

**What It Means**
In every interaction — email, call, deliverable, invoice — Roman Creative Studio shows up with the seriousness and reliability that a premium partner should. We are punctual, prepared, and present. We communicate with clarity and follow through.

**Practical Example**
A discovery call is scheduled for 10 AM. We join early, camera on, notes prepared, with a brief agenda. The client gets the experience of a studio that takes them seriously.

**Expected Behaviors**
- Be on time — for calls, deliverables, and responses
- Communicate proactively; never leave a client wondering what's happening
- Dress and present appropriately for client-facing video calls
- Every document sent externally should reflect the quality standard of the brand

**Decision Making Guidance**
If a contractor, client, or colleague saw how this was handled, would it reinforce or undermine confidence in Roman Creative Studio?

---

## Future Notes

This list should be reviewed annually. As the team grows, values should be used in hiring criteria, performance conversations, and onboarding materials. New values may be added as the company evolves — but only when they reflect something genuinely practiced, not something aspirational in name only.
