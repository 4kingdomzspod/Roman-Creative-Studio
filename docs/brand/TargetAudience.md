# Target Audience

**Roman Creative Studio Brand Bible — Vol. 1, Part A**
*Version 1.0 | Last Updated: June 2026*

---

## Purpose of This Document

This document profiles every audience Roman Creative Studio serves. Each persona defines the business size, goals, pain points, buying motivations, website needs, marketing needs, objections, and decision factors for that segment. Use this when writing copy, designing industry pages, building proposals, or training future team members.

---

## How to Use This Document

Each persona is a representative — not a stereotype. Real clients will not match every attribute exactly. Use personas to build empathy before writing, designing, or selling — not to replace listening.

## Persona 1: Dental Practices

**Business Size:** Solo practice (1 dentist) to small group (2–4 dentists)  
**Revenue Range:** $500K–$3M annually  
**Decision Maker:** Practice owner (dentist), office manager, or spouse/partner of dentist

**Goals**
- Increase new patient volume, especially fee-for-service patients
- Reduce dependence on insurance referrals
- Look as credible online as larger corporate dental chains
- Attract patients for high-value services (implants, Invisalign, cosmetic)

**Pain Points**
- Current website was built by a friend or a budget vendor and is embarrassing
- Competitors have professional sites; they don't
- Patients are researching and choosing online before ever calling
- They have no time to manage or update their digital presence
- Previous SEO companies charged monthly fees with no visible results

**Buying Motivations**
- A website that makes their practice look as good as it actually is
- More phone calls and appointment requests from the right patients
- A credible online presence they don't have to think about daily

**Website Needs**
- Trust signals: team bios, before/after galleries, patient testimonials
- Service pages for high-value procedures (implants, cosmetic, emergency)
- Online booking or appointment request form
- Mobile-optimized (patients research on phones)
- Local SEO foundation (ranks in their city)
- HIPAA-conscious design and form handling

**Marketing Needs**
- Google Business Profile optimization
- Local SEO (rank for "dentist near me" in their city)
- Review generation strategy
- Potentially: Google Ads for competitive markets

**Objections**
- "I've been burned by a web company before"
- "I don't know if a new website will actually bring in more patients"
- "We already have a website — why replace it?"
- "Can I see examples of dental websites you've done?"

**Decision Factors**
- Portfolio quality (especially dental examples)
- Professional communication in early interactions
- Clear scope and pricing — no surprises
- A clear explanation of how the site will bring in more patients

## Persona 2: Churches and Nonprofits

**Business Size:** Congregation of 50–500 members; nonprofits with 1–10 staff  
**Revenue Range:** N/A (budget-based); website projects $3K–$8K (often committee-approved)  
**Decision Maker:** Senior pastor, executive director, communications director, or board committee

**Goals**
- Reach new people in the community who are searching for a church
- Make their online presence welcoming and easy to navigate
- Communicate their mission, values, and service times clearly
- Enable online giving and event registration
- Help members stay connected between services

**Pain Points**
- Current website was built by a volunteer who has since left
- The site is impossible to update without technical help
- Visitors can't find basic information (service times, address, what to expect)
- The site doesn't reflect the warmth and quality of the actual community
- Multiple stakeholders with different opinions and limited budget

**Buying Motivations**
- A site that reflects who they are and serves their community online
- Easy to update by non-technical staff
- A site that feels welcoming to first-time visitors
- Demonstrable improvement in digital outreach

**Website Needs**
- Clear first-visit information: what to expect, what to wear, where to park
- Service times and location prominently displayed
- Online giving integration
- Sermon archive or media page
- Events calendar
- Contact and prayer request forms
- Mobile-first design

**Marketing Needs**
- Local SEO (rank for "church in [city]" and "[denomination] church near me")
- Google Business Profile
- Social media presence integration

**Objections**
- "We're a nonprofit — our budget is very limited"
- "We need approval from our leadership/board before moving forward"
- "Can the staff update this without coding knowledge?"
- "We've had websites built before and they didn't last"

**Decision Factors**
- Values alignment — they want to work with someone who understands faith communities
- Ease of maintenance (CMS or documented process)
- Clear timeline
- References or portfolio from similar organizations

## Persona 3: Local Service Businesses

**Business Size:** 1–20 employees  
**Revenue Range:** $300K–$5M annually  
**Decision Maker:** Business owner or operations manager  
**Industries:** HVAC, plumbing, landscaping, cleaning, pest control, roofing, auto repair

**Goals**
- Generate more inbound leads from Google
- Look as professional as franchise competitors
- Reduce dependence on word-of-mouth alone
- Build a scalable lead generation system

**Pain Points**
- Word-of-mouth is strong but not consistent enough to plan around
- Competitors who appear higher on Google are winning jobs they should be getting
- Their current website (if they have one) is outdated or built on a free platform
- They've tried paid ads but felt like they were wasting money

**Buying Motivations**
- More calls and form submissions from their service area
- A website that makes them look like the expert in their trade
- Showing up when local customers search for their service

**Website Needs**
- Service area pages for local SEO
- Fast, mobile-optimized experience (customers search on the job site)
- Click-to-call and quick contact form above the fold
- Trust signals: licenses, certifications, years in business, reviews
- Before/after or project gallery

**Marketing Needs**
- Local SEO (rank for "[service] in [city]")
- Google Business Profile
- Review management
- Potentially: Google Local Services Ads

**Objections**
- "I had a website built before and it didn't help"
- "I get most of my work from referrals — do I really need a website?"
- "I don't want to spend a lot of money on something I can't measure"

**Decision Factors**
- Proof that the website will bring in work
- Simple, clear pricing
- Fast turnaround
- Someone who can explain the ROI in plain language

## Persona 4: Startups and New Businesses

**Business Size:** 1–10 employees; pre-revenue to early revenue  
**Revenue Range:** Pre-revenue to $500K  
**Decision Maker:** Founder or co-founder

**Goals**
- Launch with a professional digital presence that builds immediate credibility
- Attract first customers, investors, or partners
- Establish a brand identity that can scale
- Compete with more established players in their space

**Pain Points**
- Current "website" is a landing page or Notion doc
- They know they need to look professional but have limited budget
- No brand identity beyond a logo
- Uncertainty about what they actually need vs. what's nice to have

**Buying Motivations**
- Look established even when they're new
- A solid foundation they can build on as the company grows
- Speed — they need to move fast

**Website Needs**
- Clear, compelling homepage that explains the product or service
- Strong CTA structure (sign up, book a demo, contact)
- About/Team page to build trust
- Brand identity if not yet established
- Mobile-optimized and fast-loading

**Marketing Needs**
- SEO foundation from day one
- Open Graph tags for social sharing
- Analytics implementation

**Objections**
- "We're early stage and budget is tight"
- "We'll probably redesign anyway once we raise money"
- "Can we phase this across a few months?"

**Decision Factors**
- Portfolio quality — does the work look like where they want to be?
- Flexibility on scope and phasing
- Speed of delivery
- Founder-to-founder communication style

## Persona 5: Healthcare and Medical Spas

**Business Size:** 1–5 providers  
**Revenue Range:** $500K–$5M  
**Decision Maker:** Practice owner, medical director, or office manager

**Goals**
- Attract patients for elective, high-value procedures (Botox, fillers, laser, weight loss)
- Build a premium, trustworthy online presence
- Rank locally for their specialty
- Reduce reliance on social media as the primary marketing channel

**Pain Points**
- Current website doesn't reflect the premium quality of the practice environment
- Over-reliance on Instagram with no website backup
- Patients judge the quality of care by the quality of the online presence
- Competitors with better websites are taking market share

**Website Needs**
- High-quality photography and video integration
- Treatment/service pages with detailed, educational content
- Before/after gallery with filterable categories
- Online booking integration
- Trust signals: credentials, certifications, media features
- HIPAA-conscious contact forms

**Decision Factors**
- Premium design portfolio
- Evidence of understanding medical/aesthetic space
- HIPAA awareness
- Ongoing care relationship

## Persona 6: Restaurants and Food Businesses

**Business Size:** 1–3 locations  
**Revenue Range:** $500K–$3M  
**Decision Maker:** Owner or general manager

**Goals**
- Drive reservations and online orders
- Build a distinct brand identity in a crowded market
- Show up in local search results
- Attract the right dining demographic

**Pain Points**
- Website is outdated or was built on a free platform with limited customization
- Menus are hard to update or not mobile-friendly
- Losing bookings to competitors with better online presence
- Heavy reliance on third-party platforms (Yelp, OpenTable) that take commissions

**Website Needs**
- Fast, visually compelling design (food photography-forward)
- Mobile-first (most reservations are made from phones)
- Online reservation or ordering integration
- Updatable menu (ideally PDF or easily editable section)
- Hours, location, and parking information prominent
- Events and private dining page

**Decision Factors**
- Visual design quality (they are a visual business)
- Fast turnaround
- Ease of maintenance
- Understanding of the hospitality industry

## Persona 7: Professional Services

**Business Size:** Solo to 10 employees  
**Revenue Range:** $200K–$3M  
**Decision Maker:** Founding partner or managing director  
**Industries:** Law firms, accountants, consultants, financial advisors, coaches

**Goals**
- Build authority and credibility online
- Attract the right clients — not everyone, the right ones
- Generate qualified leads from organic search
- Establish thought leadership in their specialty

**Pain Points**
- Their website looks like every other professional services website
- They're losing prospects to competitors with stronger online positioning
- They have expertise but struggle to communicate it compellingly online
- Generic website copy doesn't reflect the quality of their work

**Website Needs**
- Clear, specific service positioning
- Thought leadership content (blog, resources, guides)
- Case studies or outcomes (where allowed by industry ethics)
- Simple, elegant design that communicates seriousness
- Lead capture form with a valuable offer (consultation, audit, assessment)

**Decision Factors**
- Strategic thinking in the discovery conversation
- Clear, confident writing on the proposal and website
- Portfolio that demonstrates credibility, not just aesthetics

## Persona 8: Fitness and Wellness

**Business Size:** 1–3 locations, solo trainers to studio owners  
**Revenue Range:** $100K–$1M  
**Decision Maker:** Owner or studio director

**Goals**
- Fill class schedules and membership slots
- Build a brand that attracts a specific fitness demographic
- Create an online experience that converts visitors to members

**Pain Points**

## Persona 9: Construction and Contractors

**Business Size:** 2–30 employees  
**Revenue Range:** $500K–$10M  
**Decision Maker:** Owner or business development lead

**Goals**
- Win larger commercial or residential projects
- Look credible to general contractors and developers
- Generate inbound leads from homeowners or businesses

## Persona 10: Real Estate

**Business Size:** Solo agents to small brokerages  
**Decision Maker:** Agent, team lead, or brokerage owner

**Goals**
- Generate seller and buyer leads independent of Zillow and Realtor.com
- Build personal brand and authority in their market
- Attract referrals and repeat clients

**Pain Points**
- Dependent on third-party platforms for leads
- Personal brand website looks amateur compared to production

- Market-specific content for SEO
- Property search integration (IDX)
- Lead capture with a value offer (home valuation, buyer guide)

## Persona 11: Retail and E-Commerce

**Business Size:** 1–10 employees  
**Revenue Range:** $100K–$2M  
**Decision Maker:** Owner or e-commerce manager

**Goals**
- Increase online sales
- Reduce dependence on Amazon or Etsy
- Build a direct brand relationship with customers
- Improve site conversion rate

**Pain Points**
- High traffic, low conversion
- Current site was set up quickly and never properly optimized
- Cart abandonment is high but no system to recover it
- No clear brand identity online

**Website Needs**
- Conversion-optimized product pages
- Clear brand identity throughout
- Abandoned cart recovery
- Trust signals: reviews, return policy, secure checkout
- Email capture with offer

## Persona 12: Technology Companies

**Business Size:** 5–50 employees  
**Revenue Range:** $1M–$20M  
**Decision Maker:** CEO, CMO, or head of marketing

**Goals**
- Communicate a complex product clearly and compellingly
- Generate qualified demo requests or free trials
- Build credibility with enterprise buyers
- Stand out in a crowded SaaS or tech market

**Pain Points**
- Marketing site doesn't match the quality of the product
- Copy is too technical — doesn't speak to buyer outcomes
- Conversion rate on demo request or sign-up is low
- Design feels dated relative to competitors

**Website Needs**
- Clear, outcome-focused product positioning
- Case studies and ROI data
- Demo or trial CTA above the fold
- Comparison pages
- Trust signals: logos, testimonials, security certifications
