# Proposal System
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 5, Document 6

---

### The Purpose of a Proposal

A proposal is not an information document. It is a closing document.

Its job is not to explain what RCS does. The discovery call did that. The proposal's job is to:
1. Confirm that RCS understood the prospect's specific problem
2. Present the solution as inevitable given the problem
3. Build confidence that RCS is the right partner
4. Make the investment feel logical, not scary
5. Ask for a decision

A proposal that leaves the prospect saying "I need to think about it" has failed. A proposal that leaves them saying "this makes sense — how do I sign?" has succeeded.

---

## Section 1 — Proposal Principles

### 1.1 Proposal Rules

1. **Never send a proposal before a discovery call.** A proposal without context is a quote. A quote invites price comparison. A proposal invites partnership.

2. **Never send a proposal to someone without decision-making authority.** It dies in their inbox.

3. **Always schedule a proposal review call before sending.** The proposal is presented live — not emailed and forgotten.

4. **Keep it focused.** A proposal that covers every possible service, option, and caveat confuses. A proposal that solves one specific problem with one clear solution closes.

5. **Design it.** The proposal document is a brand touchpoint. It must match the quality of the work it represents — dark background, gold accents, Cormorant Garamond headings, Inter body, clean layout.

## Section 2 — Proposal Structure

### Section 1: Cover

```
[RCS Logo]

Prepared for: [Client Name / Business Name]
Date: [Date]

"[One-line statement of the opportunity — e.g.,
'A conversion-focused website for Coastal Dental Group']"
```

---

### Section 2: The Situation

**Headline:** "Where You Are Today"

This section mirrors back to the prospect exactly what they told RCS on the discovery call. It proves RCS listened and understood — it is not generic agency boilerplate.

**Format:**
```
[Business Name] is a [X-year-old / growing / expanding] [industry] business
serving [audience] in [location/market].

Right now, your digital presence has three specific gaps that are
creating friction in your growth:

1. [Specific problem 1 from discovery call — with business impact]
2. [Specific problem 2 from discovery call — with business impact]
3. [Specific problem 3 from discovery call — with business impact]
```

**Why this works:** The prospect sees themselves in the document. This is not a template — it is their story. That builds immediate trust and confirms that RCS pays attention.

---

### Section 3: The Opportunity

**Headline:** "What's Possible"

Reframe the problems from Section 2 as business opportunities:

```
For a [industry] business at your stage, a conversion-optimized
digital presence is not a cosmetic upgrade — it is infrastructure.

Based on what you've described, the primary opportunity is:
[Specific outcome in their language — e.g., "increasing new patient
inquiries from your website from roughly X/month to X/month within
the first 90 days post-launch."]

This is achievable. Here's how we approach it.
```

---

### Section 4: The Proposed Solution

**Headline:** "What We'd Build for You"

Describe the specific solution — not a list of deliverables, but a coherent system with a purpose:

```
What we're proposing is not a website redesign. It's a conversion
architecture built around your specific business goals.

Here's the structure:

Foundation Layer: [Brand alignment, design system setup, etc.]

Conversion Layer: [Homepage architecture, service pages, industry-specific
features, CTA strategy, trust signal placement]

Authority Layer: [SEO structure, industry pages, blog setup, schema markup]

Retention Layer: [Care plan, analytics, optimization roadmap]
```

Write this as strategy, not as a feature list. The prospect should finish this section thinking "they understand what I need."

---

### Section 5: Scope of Work

**Headline:** "Exactly What's Included"

This is the specific, unambiguous list of deliverables. Every item is clear enough that both parties know exactly what's in and what's out.

```
DESIGN & BUILD
✓ Custom website design — [X] pages
✓ Mobile, tablet, and desktop responsive
✓ [Industry-specific feature — e.g., appointment request form]
✓ [Industry-specific feature — e.g., before/after gallery]
✓ Contact form + CTA integration
✓ [Additional scope items]

SEO FOUNDATION
✓ On-page SEO structure (H1/H2, meta titles, meta descriptions)
✓ Schema markup (LocalBusiness + Service)
✓ Google Analytics 4 setup
✓ Google Search Console setup and sitemap submission

POST-LAUNCH
✓ 30-day post-launch support
✓ Training session: how to make basic content updates

NOT INCLUDED (unless added):
✗ Copywriting (unless copywriting package selected)
✗ Photography / photography direction
✗ Ongoing SEO management
✗ Pages beyond [X] count
```

**Why include "Not Included":** Prevents scope creep disputes. Sets clear professional expectations. Signals that RCS is structured and experienced.

### Section 6: The Process

**Headline:** "How We Work Together"

Show the structured process to remove fear of the unknown:

```
WEEK 1–2: DISCOVERY & STRATEGY
  Kickoff call — goals, audience, competition deep-dive
  Content collection — existing assets, photography, copy
  Strategy document — site architecture + conversion plan

WEEK 2–4: DESIGN
  Wireframes for approval — structure and flow
  Visual design mockups — homepage + key pages
  Design approval round (up to 2 revision rounds)

WEEK 4–7: BUILD & OPTIMIZE
  Development of all pages
  SEO implementation
  Mobile QA + cross-browser testing
  Staging site review

WEEK 7–8: LAUNCH
  Final client review
  DNS migration (if applicable)
  Launch + post-launch monitoring
  30-day support window begins
```

**Adapt timeline per project scope.**

### Section 7: Investment

**Headline:** "The Investment"

Never use "Cost" or "Price" in this section header.

```
TOTAL INVESTMENT

[Project Name]: $XX,XXX

PAYMENT STRUCTURE
  50% to begin work          $X,XXX
  25% at design approval     $X,XXX
  25% at launch              $X,XXX

ADD-ONS AVAILABLE
  Copywriting (all pages):   +$X,XXX
  Monthly Care Plan:         +$XXX/month
  SEO Growth Retainer:       +$X,XXX/month

INVESTMENT VALID FOR: 14 days from proposal date
```

**The 14-day validity rule:** Creates soft urgency without manipulation. A prospect who takes 3 months to decide may have a changed scope or market — pricing review is legitimate.

### Section 8: Expected Outcomes

**Headline:** "What to Expect"

Connect the investment to projected outcomes:

```
Based on comparable [industry] projects, clients who complete this
engagement with full strategy implementation typically see:

  Conversion improvement:   20–40% increase in qualified website leads
  SEO visibility:           First-page ranking for [primary industry keyword]
                            within 3–6 months
  Performance:              Page load time under 2.5 seconds
  User experience:          Mobile-first, accessible, conversion-structured

Timeline to measurable results: 60–90 days post-launch

Note: Results vary by market, competition, and marketing investment.
These projections are based on industry benchmarks, not guarantees.
```

**Never guarantee specific results.** Present benchmarks and realistic projections.

## Section 3 — Proposal Delivery Protocol

### 3.1 Always Present Live

Never email a proposal and say "let me know what you think."

The proposal review call is scheduled during the discovery call. The proposal is emailed 24–48 hours before the review call so the prospect can read it first. The review call walks through it together — answering questions and closing.

**Review call agenda:**
1. "Did you get a chance to look through it?" — gauge their reaction
2. Walk through the Situation section: "Does this accurately capture where you are?"
3. Walk through the Solution: "Does this approach make sense for your goals?"
4. Walk through the Investment: State it confidently. Pause.
5. Address questions and objections
6. Ask for commitment: "Are you ready to move forward?"

### 3.2 Proposal Follow-Up

If the prospect doesn't respond after the review call:
- 24 hours: Send follow-up (see Follow-Up System)
- 3 days: Second follow-up with one new value element
- 7 days: Final follow-up — check if circumstances have changed
- 14 days: Proposal expires. Send notification. Close the loop.

## Section 4 — Proposal Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Generic proposal not referencing their specific situation | Looks like a template; loses trust |
| Listing 15 deliverables without explaining what they mean | Confuses, doesn't close |
| Using "cost" instead of "investment" | Frames it as an expense, not a business decision |
| Including multiple pricing options without guidance | Creates decision paralysis |
| No "Not Included" section | Sets up scope creep disputes post-contract |
| Sending without scheduling a review call | Proposal dies in inbox |
| 20-page proposal | Too much to absorb; main message is diluted |
| No clear next steps / no CTA at the end | Leaves the decision open-ended |
