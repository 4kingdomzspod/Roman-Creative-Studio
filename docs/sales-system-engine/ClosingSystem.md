# Closing System
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 5, Document 9

---

### The Closing Philosophy

Closing is not a moment. It is a process that begins in the discovery call and concludes at signing.

Agencies that struggle to close usually have one of three problems:
1. They haven't qualified the prospect sufficiently
2. They haven't built enough trust and authority throughout the process
3. They treat the "close" as a single high-pressure moment rather than a natural conclusion

When the discovery call, qualification, proposal, and objection handling are done correctly — the close is not a hard push. It is a simple question: "Are you ready to move forward?"

---

## Section 1 — When to Close

### In the Discovery Call

Close to the next step — not to the sale itself:

```
"Based on everything you've told me, I think there's a strong case
for moving forward. I'll put together a proposal this week.

Let's schedule a call to review it together — does [day] at [time]
work?"
```

The discovery call closes to a proposal review call. Never to a signature. Trying to close to a contract in the first call is premature and feels high-pressure.

### In the Proposal Review Call

This is the primary closing call. After walking through the proposal:

```
"Does this all make sense? Any questions on the scope or investment?"

(Handle any objections)

"Are you ready to move forward?"
```

That's the close. Direct, non-manipulative, clear.

## Section 2 — Closing Mechanics

### 2.1 The Assumptive Close (For Warm, Ready Prospects)

For prospects who have shown high intent throughout — assume they're moving forward and state the next step:

```
"Great — here's what happens next: I'll send you the agreement
today, you sign and submit the deposit, and I'll schedule our
kickoff call for early next week."
```

The assumptive close works when all buying signals are present. It removes the hesitation of the explicit "are you in?" question.

### 2.2 The Summary Close (For Thoughtful Prospects)

Summarize the agreed-upon value before asking for commitment:

```
"Let me recap what we've covered:

You're losing qualified leads because your site isn't converting.
We've designed a solution that addresses your conversion flow,
your SEO visibility, and your industry-specific features.

The investment is $X, structured in milestones, and we can launch
in [X] weeks.

That solves the problem you described. Does that feel right?"
```

### 2.3 The Conditional Close (For Hesitant Prospects)

Use when there's a specific barrier to commitment:

```
"If we could resolve [specific concern], would you be ready to move forward?"

Example:
"If the payment structure worked for you — say 3 payments over 60 days —
would that make this the right time?"
```

If they say yes, resolve the condition and close. If they say no, the real objection is elsewhere — ask what it is.

### 2.4 The Urgency Close (Used Honestly Only)

Never manufacture fake urgency. Only use real urgency:

```
Real urgency examples:
- "I have one project spot available in [month] — after that I'm
  booked until [month]. If your timeline matters, now is the time."
- "The proposal pricing is held for 14 days from [date]."
- "You mentioned you want to launch before [their event/season/campaign].
  To hit that, we'd need to start by [date]."
```

**Never:** Fake deadlines, fake limited availability, artificial discounts expiring.

## Section 3 — How to Ask for Commitment

### 3.1 The Direct Ask

Most salespeople avoid asking directly for fear of hearing "no." This avoidance creates ambiguity that kills deals.

Ask directly:
```
"Are you ready to move forward?"
"Does this give you what you need to make a decision?"
"What would it take to get started this week?"
```

### 3.2 After Asking — Silence

After asking for commitment, stop talking. The next person who speaks loses negotiating position.

If the prospect is silent — they are deciding. Let them decide. The moment you fill that silence with a concession, justification, or discount, you've taught them that silence is a negotiating tactic.

### 3.3 When They Say Yes

```
"Excellent. I'll send you the agreement and invoice today.
Once that's signed and the deposit is received, I'll email
you within 24 hours to schedule the kickoff call."
```

Move immediately. Don't over-celebrate, don't over-explain. They said yes. Act on it.

## Section 4 — Avoiding Indecision Loops

An indecision loop is when a prospect keeps delaying without ever committing or declining. It is the most time-wasting scenario in sales.

### Identifying the Loop

Signs you're in an indecision loop:
- More than 3 follow-ups without a clear answer
- Repeated "we need a bit more time" responses
- Rescheduled calls that keep getting rescheduled
- Engagement without commitment for more than 3 weeks post-proposal

### Breaking the Loop

Name it directly:
```
"[First Name], I want to be respectful of both our time.

It's been [X weeks] since we reviewed the proposal, and I'm getting
the sense the timing isn't right — or there's a concern I haven't
been able to address.

I'd rather have a straight conversation than keep following up.
What's actually standing in the way?"
```

This forces a real answer. Either the real objection surfaces — which can be handled — or they admit it's not happening, which saves everyone's time.

### The Closing Door

If the loop continues after the direct conversation:
```
"I understand — and I don't want to pressure you. I'm going to
close out this proposal on [date] and re-open it when the timing
works for you.

If things shift, I'm one email away."
```

Closing the loop is not losing the deal. It is protecting your time and maintaining professional dignity. Many "closed" prospects come back 3–6 months later when the timing is right.

## Section 5 — What Kills Deals

| Deal Killer | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sending a proposal without a review call | Prospect reads it alone, gets confused, never responds | Always present live |
| Over-explaining after stating the price | Signals insecurity in the number | State the price, then pause |
| Dropping price unprompted | Destroys perceived value instantly | Never discount without scope change |
| Not asking for commitment | The deal can't close if you never ask | "Are you ready to move forward?" |
| Too many follow-up emails with no value | Trains them to ignore you | Every follow-up adds something |
| Letting weeks go by without a follow-up | Prospect moves on or finds another agency | Follow-up system in writing |
| Accepting "I'll think about it" without clarity | Enters indecision loop | Ask: "What specifically are you thinking through?" |
