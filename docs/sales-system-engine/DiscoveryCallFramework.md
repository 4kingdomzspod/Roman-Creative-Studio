# Discovery Call Framework
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 5, Document 3

---

### The Nature of the Discovery Call

The discovery call is not a getting-to-know-you conversation. It is a structured diagnostic session with a clear objective: determine whether RCS can deliver a measurable business result for this prospect, and if so, move toward a proposal.

The call has three phases:
1. **Diagnosis** — understand the problem at business depth, not surface level
2. **Prescription** — position RCS's approach as the specific solution
3. **Direction** — close to next step (proposal or qualified rejection)

**Call duration:** 30–45 minutes
**Call format:** Video call (Zoom, Google Meet) — camera on for both parties
**Who should be on the call:** Decision-maker. If the decision-maker isn't present, reschedule.

---

## Section 1 — Pre-Call Preparation

Before every discovery call, complete the following:

**Research checklist (10–15 minutes):**
- [ ] Visit their current website — note 3 specific observations (what works, what's broken)
- [ ] Check their Google Business Profile — reviews, photos, rating
- [ ] Search their industry keyword in their city — see where they rank
- [ ] Check their social media — activity level, content quality, follower engagement
- [ ] Note their primary service offerings and apparent target customer

**Why:** Walking into the call with specific observations about their business signals preparation, expertise, and genuine interest. It separates RCS from every agency that shows up with a generic pitch.

**Pre-call message (sent 24 hours before):**
```
Looking forward to our call tomorrow, [First Name].

To make the most of our time, it would help to know:
1. What's the #1 thing your website should be doing for your business
   that it currently isn't?
2. What's your timeline for making a change?

See you tomorrow at [time].

— Alexander
```

This primes the prospect to arrive thinking about outcomes, not features. It also surfaces disqualifiers early (if they answer "just want something that looks nice" — expectation-setting is needed).

## Section 2 — Call Flow

### Phase 1: Open (3–5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Establish control of the frame, set expectations, build comfort.

**Script:**
```
"[First Name], great to connect. Before we dive in — here's what I want
to accomplish in the next 30 minutes:

I want to understand your business, what's happening with your digital
presence right now, and where you want to be. From there, I can tell you
honestly whether we're a fit and what I'd recommend.

I'm not going to pitch you — this is about figuring out if there's a
real opportunity here. Does that work?"
```

**Why this opener works:**
- Takes control without being aggressive
- Removes the prospect's guard ("not a pitch")
- Sets a diagnostic tone, not a selling tone
- Creates permission for honest conversation

---

### Phase 2: Business Discovery (8–12 minutes)

**Purpose:** Understand the business at depth. The more you understand about their business, the more specific and valuable your prescription will be.

**Questions to ask (choose 4–5, don't read from a list):**

**Business context:**
- "Tell me about [Business Name] — what do you do and who do you serve?"
- "How long have you been in business? How has the business grown?"
- "What's your primary way of getting new [patients/clients/members] right now?"

**Current digital situation:**
- "When you look at your website today, what's it doing well — and what's clearly not working?"
- "How many leads or inquiries do you get from your website per month roughly?"
- "When a potential [client/patient] finds you online, what do you want them to do?"

**Business goals:**
- "If we were talking a year from now and everything went right — what would your digital presence look like?"
- "Is growth the goal, or is it more about professionalism and positioning?"
- "Are there competitors in your space whose online presence you respect? Who and why?"

**Listening priorities:**
- What specific result are they chasing? (more leads, better positioning, specific campaign)
- What's the cost of NOT solving this? (missed leads, losing to competitors, embarrassment)
- What have they tried already? (DIY, previous agency, templates)

**Rule:** Listen more than you talk in this phase. Every detail they share is ammunition for a precise prescription in Phase 3.

---

### Phase 3: Gap Identification (5–7 minutes)

**Purpose:** Reflect back what you've heard — with professional analysis added. This is where RCS demonstrates expertise.

**Structure:**
```
"Based on what you've told me — and from looking at your website before
this call — here's what I'm seeing:

[PROBLEM 1]: Your site doesn't have [specific element], which means
[specific consequence for their business]. For a [their industry] business,
that's typically costing you [specific impact].

[PROBLEM 2]: From an SEO standpoint, when I searched '[their service]
in [their area]', you're not appearing on page 1. That means [specific
number] of potential [patients/clients] per month are finding your
competitor instead.

[PROBLEM 3]: The conversion flow on your site doesn't guide visitors
toward action. There's no clear next step, no trust signals above the
fold, and the CTA isn't specific enough. Visitors land and leave."
```

**Rules:**
- Be specific. Vague analysis sounds like a sales pitch. Specific analysis sounds like expertise.
- Reference actual things you saw on their site in your pre-call research.
- Frame each problem in terms of business impact (lost leads, lost revenue, competitive disadvantage).
- Do not soften your assessment to avoid discomfort. Honest diagnosis builds trust.

**Confirmation check:**
```
"Does that match what you're experiencing? Am I missing anything?"
```

This invites them to add detail and signals that you're listening, not pitching.

---

### Phase 4: Solution Positioning (5–7 minutes)

**Purpose:** Describe RCS's approach in terms of their specific problem. Not a general pitch — a tailored prescription.

**Structure:**
```
"Here's how I'd approach this:

For a [their industry] business at your stage, the priority isn't
just design — it's architecture. We'd build the site around a
clear conversion flow: [describe 2–3 specific structural decisions
relevant to their situation].

Because you mentioned [specific goal they stated], we'd also
prioritize [specific strategy — SEO, trust signals, industry-specific
feature] early in the process.

From what you've told me, I'd estimate a well-built site in your
industry generates roughly [specific outcome] within [timeframe].
We've seen this pattern across [dental/church/healthcare] clients."
```

**Rule:** Do not present a solution before completing Phases 2 and 3. You cannot prescribe before you diagnose.

---

### Phase 5: Investment Preview (3–5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Introduce pricing at a conceptual level before sending a proposal. Surface budget alignment before investing time in proposal development.

**Script:**
```
"Before I put together a proposal — I want to make sure we're in the
same ballpark on investment, so we don't waste each other's time.

For what you've described — [brief summary of scope] — a project like
this typically ranges from $[low anchor] to $[high anchor], depending
on scope and timeline.

Is that a range your business can work with?"
```

**If yes:** Move to next steps (proposal).
**If hesitation:** Do not drop price. Explore the gap (see Objection Handling System, Document 7).
**If clear no:** "I appreciate your honesty. There are great options at lower investment levels — they just won't have the strategic depth we bring. I don't want to waste your time or ours."

---

### Phase 6: Objection Handling (if needed, 2–5 minutes)

See ObjectionHandlingSystem.md for full framework.

The most common discovery call objection is price. The response is never a discount — it is a reframe to ROI.

---

### Phase 7: Close to Next Step (2–3 minutes)

**Purpose:** Do not end the call without a defined next step. Ambiguity kills deals.

**If qualified and budget-aligned:**
```
"Here's what I'd recommend: I'll put together a proposal this week
that outlines exactly what we'd build, the process, timeline, and
investment. I'll send it by [specific day].

I'd want to walk you through it together — can we schedule a 30-minute
call for [2–3 days after proposal send date] to review it?"
```

**Always schedule the proposal review call BEFORE ending the discovery call.**
Never send a proposal and say "let me know what you think." The proposal dies in their inbox.

**If not yet qualified but has potential:**
```
"I think there's something here, but I want to make sure we're
the right fit. [Specific question about budget/timeline/authority].

Let me put together a few thoughts and send them over — and we can
decide from there if it makes sense to move forward."
```

**If disqualified:**
```
"I want to be straight with you, [First Name] — based on what you've
described, I don't think now is the right time for a project at our
level. [Specific reason — budget, stage, timeline].

That doesn't mean never. When [condition changes], I'd love to revisit.
Is it okay if I stay in touch?"
```
