# Scaling Roadmap

**Owner:** Alexander Roman / CEO  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-01  
**Status:** Vision Defined — Currently at Stage 1

---

## Purpose

Map the full growth trajectory of Roman Creative Studio from solo founder to multi-brand company. Define the goals, team, revenue, processes, technology, documentation, risks, and success criteria at each stage. This is the long-term vision document.

---

## Business Value

Growth without a map is wandering. This roadmap ensures every decision — about hiring, products, pricing, technology, and marketing — is evaluated in the context of where RCS is now and where it's going. It prevents premature scaling and prevents under-investing when the time is right.

---

## How to Use This Document

1. Identify which stage RCS is currently in (see Current Status)
2. Review the success criteria for the current stage
3. Do not attempt Stage N+2 activities while still in Stage N
4. Review this document quarterly and update Current Status
5. When all success criteria for a stage are met, formally advance to next stage

**Current Status:** Stage 1 — Solo Founder (as of 2026-07-01)

---

## Stage 1 — Solo Founder

### Vision
Establish RCS as a legitimate, professional digital agency with a polished brand, operational systems, first paying clients, and predictable recurring revenue.

### Goals
- Build the brand and website to premium agency standards
- Land first 3 paying clients
- Sign first Care Plan subscriber
- Establish business systems and documentation
- Validate service-market fit

### Team Size
- 1 (Alexander, doing everything)

### Revenue Targets
- Monthly Revenue: $0 → $5,000/mo
- MRR: $0 → $500/mo
- Annual Revenue: <$60,000

### Processes
- Manual lead tracking in HubSpot Free
- Manual invoicing via Stripe
- Manual reporting via Google Docs
- Manual project tracking in Notion or spreadsheet
- All client communication via email

### Technology
- Website: GitHub Pages (static HTML)
- Forms: Formspree
- Email: Resend (transactional) + MailerLite (marketing)
- CRM: HubSpot Free
- Payments: Stripe
- Scheduling: Calendly
- Analytics: GA4 + Search Console + Clarity
- Docs: Google Workspace

### Documentation
- All Phase 8A–8C documents complete
- Service agreements and proposal templates
- Onboarding and project management SOPs
- Client communication templates

### Risks
- Revenue dependency on few clients
- Founder burnout from doing everything
- Long sales cycles with no pipeline
- No recurring revenue safety net

### Success Criteria
- [ ] 3+ completed projects
- [ ] $3,000+ monthly revenue for 2 consecutive months
- [ ] 2+ active Care Plan subscribers
- [ ] NPS/CSAT score of 8+/10 average
- [ ] GA4 and CRM fully operational
- [ ] Complete document system (Phases 1–8C) in place

## Stage 2 — Freelancer

### Vision
Grow to consistent, predictable revenue with contractor support. Prove that RCS can deliver quality work with external help. Establish a referral engine.

### Goals
- Scale to $10,000+/month consistently
- Build first contractor relationship (designer or developer)
- Achieve 5+ Care Plan subscribers
- Generate 30%+ of leads from referrals
- Publish consistent content (1 blog post/month, 2 podcast episodes/month)

### Team Size
- 1 full-time (Alexander)
- 1 part-time contractor (project-based)

### Revenue Targets
- Monthly Revenue: $5,000 → $12,000/mo
- MRR: $500 → $2,000/mo
- Annual Revenue: $60,000 → $120,000

### Processes
- Contractor onboarding SOP
- Contractor brief and handoff system
- Quality review process for contractor work
- Automated lead nurture sequences (MailerLite)
- Monthly client reports delivered on schedule

### Technology
- Add: Supabase (database for portal groundwork)
- Add: Zapier (workflow automation)
- Add: Ahrefs or SEMrush (SEO tracking)
- Update: Calendly to Teams plan
- Consider: Notion for internal KB (or continue with docs)

### Documentation
- Contractor onboarding guide
- Brand standards brief for contractors
- Code standards guide for dev contractors
- Quality review checklist

### Risks
- Contractor quality inconsistency
- Client expectations set by founder may not be met by contractor
- Revenue plateau at capacity ceiling
- Contractor dependency (single point of failure)

### Success Criteria
- [ ] $10,000+ monthly revenue for 3 consecutive months
- [ ] $2,000+ MRR
- [ ] 1+ contractor relationship established
- [ ] 30%+ leads from referrals
- [ ] Client portal architecture complete (even if not launched)

## Stage 3 — Small Agency

### Vision
Transform from freelancer-with-help to a true agency with defined roles, structured delivery, and scalable systems. Launch the client portal. Begin building an audience.

### Goals
- $25,000+/month revenue
- 15+ active Care Plan subscribers
- Client portal launched and in use
- Internal dashboard operational
- Hire first part-time employee (operations/admin)
- 1,000+ email subscribers

### Team Size
- 1 full-time (Alexander — strategy, sales, creative direction)
- 1 part-time employee (operations/admin)
- 2–3 freelance contractors (design, development, content)

### Revenue Targets
- Monthly Revenue: $12,000 → $25,000/mo
- MRR: $2,000 → $8,000/mo
- Annual Revenue: $150,000 → $250,000

### Processes
- Client portal fully operational (onboarding, project tracking, invoicing)
- Internal dashboard operational
- AI-assisted monthly client reports (automated)
- Weekly team stand-up (even if async)
- Formal proposal system with digital signature
- Formal contract system

### Technology
- Launch: `portal.romancreativestudio.co` (Next.js + Supabase)
- Launch: `admin.romancreativestudio.co` (internal dashboard)
- Add: DocuSign or PandaDoc (contracts + proposals)
- Add: QuickBooks (accounting)
- Upgrade: Supabase to Pro plan
- Add: Anthropic Claude API (AI automation)

### Documentation
- All process documents updated for multi-person team
- Employee onboarding guide
- Role descriptions for each team member
- Meeting cadence and communication standards

### Risks
- Growing too fast before systems are ready
- Client portal launch delays impacting Care Plan retention
- Overhead costs rising faster than revenue
- Alexander still bottleneck on all decisions

### Success Criteria
- [ ] $20,000+ monthly revenue for 3 consecutive months
- [ ] $5,000+ MRR
- [ ] Client portal live with 5+ active client users
- [ ] Part-time employee or contractor team established
- [ ] 1,000+ email subscribers
- [ ] AI report automation operational

## Stage 4 — Growing Team

### Vision
Build a team where Alexander focuses on strategy, sales, and creative direction — not execution. Delivery is handled by the team. Revenue is consistent and growing. First product or template revenue appears.

### Goals
- $50,000+/month revenue
- $15,000+ MRR
- Full team: designer, developer, account manager
- First digital product revenue (templates or course)
- Recognized in target markets (speaking, press, referrals)

### Team Size
- 3–4 full-time employees
- 2–3 contractors
- Total team: 5–7 people

### Revenue Targets
- Monthly Revenue: $25,000 → $60,000/mo
- MRR: $8,000 → $20,000/mo
- Annual Revenue: $400,000 → $600,000
- Product Revenue: First $10,000–$50,000

### Processes
- Full project management system (PM software)
- Account management role established
- Sales process delegated to account manager (partially)
- HR processes: performance reviews, benefits, PTO
- Monthly all-team meeting
- Quarterly planning sessions

### Technology
- Add: Project management tool (Linear, Asana, or Basecamp)
- Add: Slack or team communication platform
- Add: Payroll software (Gusto, Rippling)
- Add: Benefits administration
- Upgrade: All tool plans to support team size

### Risks
- Management overhead consuming Alexander's time
- Culture and quality dilution as team grows
- Revenue per employee ratio declining
- Project capacity management becomes complex

### Success Criteria
- [ ] $50,000+ monthly revenue for 3 consecutive months
- [ ] $15,000+ MRR
- [ ] Team delivering projects without Alexander in execution
- [ ] First digital product generating revenue
- [ ] Revenue per employee above $100,000

## Stage Progression Rules

1. Do not formally advance until all Success Criteria for current stage are met
2. Review stage status quarterly
3. It is acceptable to work on Stage N+1 preparation while still in Stage N
4. It is not acceptable to incur Stage N+2 costs while in Stage N
5. Some stages may merge or accelerate based on unexpected opportunities — document the decision
