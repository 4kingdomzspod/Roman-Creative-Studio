# Discovery Call

## Purpose

The discovery call is a **mutual evaluation** — we're assessing fit as much as the prospect is. The goal is not to sell. The goal is to understand the client's situation well enough to know if we can genuinely help them, and if so, to scope a solution that solves their actual problem.

**A good discovery call results in either a clear proposal opportunity or a graceful, no-hard-feelings decision that it's not the right fit.**

---

## Before the Call

- [ ] Review their current website
- [ ] Check their Google Business Profile and reviews
- [ ] Look at 1–2 competitors in their market
- [ ] Note any obvious issues (mobile broken, slow load, no CTA, etc.)
- [ ] Review the intake form they submitted

---

## Call Structure (30 minutes)

### Opening (3 min)
Brief introduction. Confirm the agenda. Let them know the call is to understand their situation and see if we're a good fit — no pressure either way.

### Their Situation (10 min)
Open-ended questions to understand context:

1. "Tell me about your business — what do you do and who do you serve?"
2. "What's not working right now with your online presence?"
3. "What's the primary goal for this project? What does success look like in 6 months?"
4. "What's driving the timing on this — is there a deadline or urgency?"
5. "Have you worked with a designer or agency before? What was that experience like?"

### Their Goals (8 min)
Dig into the specific outcome they want:

6. "If the website was performing exactly as you wanted, what would be different for your business?"
7. "Who are your ideal customers and what makes them choose you over a competitor?"
8. "What's one thing competitors do online that you wish you were doing?"
9. "Are there specific features or pages you know you need?"

### Fit and Budget (7 min)
Get clear on the practical realities:

10. "Who else is involved in making this decision?"
11. "What's your budget range for this project?" (Get a number — don't accept 'depends')
12. "What's your timeline?"
13. "Do you have existing brand assets — logo, photos, copy — or do those need to be created?"

### Close (2 min)
Summarize what you heard. Confirm next steps. Set timeline for proposal.

> *"Based on what you've shared, I have a good picture of what you need. I'll put together a proposal that outlines the scope, timeline, and investment. You can expect it within [X] days. Does that work?"*

---

## Red Flags During the Call

- Refuses to give a budget ("it depends on what you propose") — push back gently
- Multiple decision-makers who aren't on the call — ask to involve them before investing in a proposal
- Unrealistic timeline with non-negotiable deadline
- "I just need something simple and cheap"
- Has worked with 3+ agencies in the past year with complaints about each

---

## After the Call

- Send a brief follow-up email within 2 hours (see FollowUpEmails.md)
- Create proposal within agreed timeframe (usually 3–5 business days)
- If not a fit: send a kind, honest note explaining why and offering an alternative resource where possible
