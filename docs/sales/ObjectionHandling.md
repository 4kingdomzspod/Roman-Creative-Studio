# Objection Handling

## Overview

Every objection is either a question in disguise or a signal that we haven't communicated value clearly enough. Handle objections by: acknowledging, clarifying, and reframing — never arguing.

---

## Common Objections

### "It's too expensive."

**What they really mean:** "I'm not convinced it's worth it" or "I can get this cheaper somewhere else."

**Response:**
> "I understand. Can I ask — what's your expectation of what a site like this would cost? ... [Hear the answer] ... The price reflects what it takes to build something that actually performs for your business. A $1,500 template site won't bring you the patients/clients you're looking for — and you'll likely end up rebuilding it. Let me show you what's included and why it's priced this way."

If they truly can't afford it: refer them to a lower-cost option with respect and close the door gracefully.

---

### "I need to think about it."

**What they really mean:** "I'm not convinced yet" or "I need to talk to someone else."

**Response:**
> "Of course. What's the main thing you'd want to think through? I'd rather answer it now than have you wondering. Is it the price, the scope, the timing, or something else?"

Identify the real objection behind the stall and address it directly.

---

### "I can do this myself / I have someone who can do it."

**Response:**
> "That's a real option. The question is whether their time and skills match what you need the site to accomplish. A lot of businesses have a site built by a nephew or a Fiverr designer and find that after a year it's not generating any business. What matters is whether the outcome matches your goals."

---

### "Can you do it cheaper?"

**Response:**
> "I can look at reducing scope to meet a lower budget. But I'm not going to do the full project for less — that would mean delivering something I'm not proud of. What I can do is propose a Phase 1 that gets you the core essentials now, with Phase 2 to add [features] once you've seen the results."

Always reduce scope, never just the price.

---

### "I worked with an agency before and it was a bad experience."

**Response:**
> "That's really common, and I'm sorry you went through it. Can you tell me what happened? ... [Listen fully] ... Here's how we work differently: [specific contrast to their experience]. I'll also show you exactly what the process looks like so you know what to expect at every stage."

---

### "How do I know this will actually work?"

**Response:**
> "Honestly, no one can guarantee specific results — anyone who does is overpromising. What I can show you is [portfolio examples], what we've done for [similar business type], and the specific things we build in that drive results. The strategy is proven; results depend on execution, which is what we control."

---

### "I need it in 2 weeks."

**Response:**
> "I understand there's urgency. A project at our quality level takes [X weeks] minimum to do properly. What I can do is prioritize your intake and get us moving immediately once the contract is signed. Rushing design tends to hurt the business more than a 2-week delay — let's make sure we get this right."
