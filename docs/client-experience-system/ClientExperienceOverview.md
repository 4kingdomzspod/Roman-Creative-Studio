## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 6, Document 1

## Section 1 — Delivery Philosophy

### 1.1 The Core Belief

Every client who works with RCS should finish the engagement thinking:
> "That was the most professional project experience I've ever had."

Not just "the website looks great" — but "the *process* was exceptional." These are different standards. A beautiful website built through a chaotic, stressful process produces a dissatisfied client who doesn't refer. A beautiful website built through a structured, confident, transparent process produces an advocate.

The product is the website. The experience is the relationship. Both must be premium.

### 1.2 What "Premium Experience" Means at RCS

**Before the project starts:**
- Client knows exactly what they signed up for
- Timeline is in writing with named milestones
- All roles and responsibilities are clear (what RCS does, what the client must provide)
- Communication channel is established

**During the project:**
- Client receives proactive updates at each milestone — no need to chase
- Approvals happen at defined gates — not ad-hoc
- Revisions are structured and tracked — not a chaotic email thread
- Any delays or changes are communicated in advance — never as a surprise

**At and after launch:**
- Client knows how to use their new website
- Client has a Care Plan option to maintain and grow it
- Client feels equipped, not dependent
- Client is asked for feedback and a testimonial at the right moment

### 1.3 Consistency Principle

Every client receives the same *process quality*, regardless of project size.

A $6,000 Foundation project and a $20,000 Authority project follow the same phases, the same communication rhythm, the same QA checklist, and the same launch protocol. The scope differs. The experience standard does not.

## Section 2 — Project Roles & Responsibilities

### 2.1 RCS Responsibilities

| Responsibility | Standard |
|---|---|
| Deliver scope exactly as written in contract | No additions or omissions without change order |
| Communicate at every milestone proactively | No client should ever have to ask "where are we?" |
| Respond to client messages within 24 business hours | Non-negotiable response standard |
| Complete and document QA before any staging review | No exceptions |
| Deliver training session before handover | Every project |
| Flag delays at least 48 hours in advance | Never surprise the client |
| Maintain project documentation throughout | For reference and dispute prevention |

### 2.2 Client Responsibilities

Communicated clearly at kickoff and in the project timeline:

| Responsibility | Standard |
|---|---|
| Complete questionnaire within 48 hours of request | Project cannot begin without it |
| Provide all required assets (logos, photos, copy) by agreed dates | Delays on client side extend timeline |
| Provide consolidated feedback within 48 hours of each review | Scattered or delayed feedback delays delivery |
| Designate one point of contact for approvals | Multiple reviewers without consensus cause revision loops |
| Have decision-maker available for milestone approvals | Not a delegate who can't say yes |

### 2.3 The "Client Delay" Rule

If a client delays providing required assets or feedback by more than 5 business days beyond the agreed date, the project timeline shifts accordingly — day-for-day. This is communicated in the kickoff document and in the contract.

This rule protects RCS from:
- Absorbing delays caused by the client
- Being blamed for missed timelines caused by late approvals
- Having open projects stretch indefinitely

## Section 3 — Experience Design Principles

### The 5 Principles of RCS Client Experience

**1. Proactive over Reactive**
Never wait for the client to ask what's happening. Send the update first.

**2. Documented over Verbal**
Every important decision, change, approval, and feedback round is documented in writing. Verbal agreements do not exist in project delivery.

**3. Structured over Improvised**
Every phase follows the documented workflow. No "I'll figure it out as I go." The system handles the process so creative energy can go into the work.

**4. Transparent over Comfortable**
If there is a delay, a problem, or a misalignment — name it directly and proactively. Transparency is more comfortable long-term than managed silence.

**5. Empowering over Dependent**
At the end of every project, the client can operate their website independently. Dependency is not a retention strategy. Genuine value is.
