# Referral Experience System
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 6, Document 12

---

### The Referral Timing Principle

Referrals requested too early produce reluctant testimonials and weak referrals.

The moment a client's website goes live, their excitement is high — but they have no results yet. A testimonial written at launch says "the process was great." A testimonial written 60 days after launch says "we increased patient inquiries by 30% and the site looks incredible." One of these converts. The other is polite noise.

**Rule:** The referral and testimonial moment happens after visible success — not immediately after launch.

## Section 1 — Satisfaction Measurement System

### 1.1 When to Measure

| Checkpoint | Timing | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Post-launch check-in | 30 days after launch | Direct conversation (call or email) |
| Results check-in | 60–90 days after launch | Email with analytics context |
| Referral / testimonial ask | At first reported result | Direct, personal ask |
| Annual satisfaction check | Every 12 months (Care Plan clients) | Short survey + call |

### 1.2 The 30-Day Check-In

```
Subject: [Business Name] — 30 Days Live — How's It Going?

Hi [First Name],

It's been 30 days since we launched [domain.com].

A few things I'm seeing from my side:
[1–2 specific analytics observations — traffic, top page, form submissions]

Two quick questions:
  1. How has the reception been from your clients / team?
  2. Is there anything you've noticed that you'd want to address?

Your included support window closes shortly. I'll be in touch
about Care Plan options that keep the site growing.

Alexander
```

### 1.3 The 60–90 Day Results Check-In

This is the referral activation trigger. By this point, the client has real data.

```
Subject: [Business Name] — 90-Day Check-In

Hi [First Name],

It's been about 3 months since launch. Here's what the data shows:

  Visitors:          [X] (up [X%] from pre-launch estimate)
  Top traffic page:  [Page name]
  Form submissions:  [X] this month
  Google ranking:    You're now on [page X] for "[keyword]"

[One specific result or observation that's most meaningful to them.]

I'd love to ask you something: would you be willing to share
a quick testimonial about your experience working with us?

And — if you know anyone in [industry / adjacent industry]
who could benefit from the same results, an introduction
would mean a lot.

Either way — it's been a genuine pleasure building this with you.

Alexander
```

## Section 2 — Testimonial Collection System

### 2.1 The Testimonial Ask

Done in the 60–90 day check-in email (above) or on a call when the client expresses satisfaction spontaneously.

**On a call:**
```
"I'm really glad it's performing this way. Can I ask a favor?
Would you be willing to write 2–3 sentences about your experience
for our website? Specifically what result you've seen and what
working with us was like.

I'll send you a quick prompt — takes about 5 minutes."
```

### 2.2 Testimonial Prompt (Sent via Email)

```
Subject: Quick Testimonial — 2–3 Sentences

Hi [First Name],

Thank you for agreeing to share your experience.

Here are 3 optional questions to guide you — answer one
or all three, in your own words:

  1. What problem were you trying to solve when you came to RCS?
  2. What specific result or improvement have you seen since launch?
  3. What was your experience working with Roman Creative Studio like?

No need to be formal — just honest. 2–4 sentences is perfect.

With your permission, I'd love to use your name, title,
and business name alongside the quote.

Reply here and I'll take care of the rest.

Thank you,
Alexander
```

### 2.3 Testimonial Approval

Before publishing any testimonial:
1. Send the final formatted quote to the client for approval
2. Get explicit written confirmation ("Yes, that's accurate and you can use it")
3. File approval email with project documentation

**Never publish a testimonial without written approval.**

### 2.4 Testimonial Quality Standards

From Phase 5 ReferralSystem — a testimonial must include:
- Real name
- Business name or title
- Specific outcome (not generic praise)

**If the client submits a generic testimonial ("Great work!"):**
```
"Thank you! To make it most useful, would it be possible to
include one specific result? Even something like 'we've already
gotten X inquiries' or 'the team loves how it looks' makes
a big difference for future clients reading it."
```

## Section 3 — Case Study Creation System

### 3.1 When to Create a Case Study

Create a case study when:
- Client has a specific, quantifiable result (metric improvement)
- Client has given written consent
- The project represents a vertical or use case RCS wants to showcase

Don't create a case study just because the project was completed. Wait for the result.

### 3.2 Case Study Consent Request

```
Subject: Case Study — [Business Name]

Hi [First Name],

We've gotten a few inquiries from other [dental/church/healthcare]
businesses, and I think your results would resonate strongly.

Would you be open to us featuring [Business Name] as a case study?

Here's what that looks like:
  - We write it (you don't have to)
  - You review and approve before anything is published
  - Includes: your challenge, our approach, and your results
  - Used on our website portfolio section
  - Could include a before/after screenshot if you're comfortable

We can include or exclude your business name — your call.

Let me know if you're open to it.

Alexander
```

### 3.3 Case Study Structure

From Phase 4 TrustAuthoritySystem — every case study follows:

```
1. CLIENT + CONTEXT
   Who they are. What industry. What stage.

2. THE CHALLENGE
   Specific problem before RCS. In their language.

3. THE RCS APPROACH
   Strategy decisions. Why we did what we did.

4. THE RESULTS
   Quantified outcomes. Real numbers. Real timeframes.

5. CLIENT TESTIMONIAL
   Direct quote from the client.

6. CTA
   "Ready for results like this? Book a free discovery call."
```

### 3.4 Case Study Approval Process

1. Draft the case study internally
2. Send full draft to client with: "Please review for accuracy and anything you'd like adjusted"
3. Receive written approval ("Approved to publish")
4. File approval with project documentation
5. Publish to `/portfolio/[slug]/`
6. Notify client when it's live (they often share it)

## Section 4 — The Full Post-Project Experience Arc

```
DAY 1 (Launch):
  Go-live confirmation email
  30-day support window begins

DAY 5–7:
  Training session completed
  Recording + reference guide delivered

DAY 30:
  30-day check-in
  Early analytics review
  Care Plan offer

DAY 60–90:
  Results check-in
  Testimonial request (if results are visible)
  Referral ask (warm, personal)
  Case study invitation (if applicable)

MONTHLY (Care Plan clients):
  Performance report by 5th of each month
  Updates completed as needed

QUARTERLY (Professional + Growth):
  Strategy call
  New opportunities identified

ANNUALLY:
  Full website audit
  Relationship review
  New project opportunities surfaced
```

## Section 5 — The Relationship Principle

The referral system is not a tactic. It is the natural outcome of a well-delivered project.

Clients who felt respected, informed, and genuinely helped refer others without being asked. The referral system simply ensures that RCS:
1. Creates enough touchpoints to stay top-of-mind
2. Asks at the right moment with the right framing
3. Makes the act of referring as easy as possible
4. Honors referrals with the same quality the original client received

> "The best marketing is a client who talks about you."
> The best client experience makes talking about you feel natural.
