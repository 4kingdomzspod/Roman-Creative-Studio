## Purpose

Translate annual goals into 90-day sprints. Quarterly planning is the primary execution cadence at RCS — it's the rhythm that keeps the company moving without waiting for an annual review to course-correct.

**Business Value:** A quarter is long enough to complete meaningful work and short enough to adjust direction if something isn't working. Quarterly planning prevents the "planning vs. doing" trap where teams spend too much time on strategy and too little on execution.

---

## The 90-Day Sprint Philosophy

- 13 weeks = enough time to launch, learn, and iterate
- Quarterly OKRs create urgency without crisis
- End-of-quarter reviews catch problems before they become annual failures
- Quarterly planning meetings force cross-department coordination

## Quarterly Planning Session Agenda

**Duration:** 3–4 hours (Stage 2+), 2 hours (Stage 1 solo)

**Participants:** CEO + all department heads

### Part 1: Prior Quarter Review (60 min)

**1A: OKR Scorecard**
- Grade each Key Result: 0.0 – 1.0
  - 1.0 = Fully achieved
  - 0.7 = Substantially achieved (acceptable)
  - 0.5 = Partial progress
  - 0.0–0.4 = Failed to achieve
- Grade each Objective based on KR averages
- Overall company score

**1B: What We Completed**
- All projects launched or delivered
- Products shipped
- Hires made
- Systems built

**1C: What We Didn't Complete**
- What was planned but not done?
- Was it a priority problem (wrong priority), a capacity problem (not enough time), or an execution problem (started but stalled)?
- Does it carry forward or get dropped?

**1D: Key Learnings**
- 3 things that went better than expected
- 3 things that went worse than expected
- 1 thing we will do differently next quarter

---

### Part 2: Context Setting (30 min)

**Financial Update**
- Revenue vs. plan (quarter)
- MRR vs. plan
- Cash position
- Any financial constraints or windfalls that affect Q planning?

**Market Update**
- Any client feedback patterns?
- Any competitive changes?
- Any technology shifts affecting our services?

**Team Update**
- Capacity for the quarter (vacations, part-time contractors, known gaps)
- Any hiring to complete this quarter?
- Any skills development needed?

---

### Part 3: Quarter OKR Setting (90 min)

**3A: Company-Level OKRs for the Quarter**
- 2–4 objectives maximum
- 2–4 key results per objective
- Must connect directly to annual OKRs
- Assign owners to each objective

**3B: Department-Level OKRs**
- Each department head proposes their department OKRs
- Leadership team reviews for alignment and feasibility
- Identify dependencies across departments

**3C: Project Planning**
- Which client projects are active this quarter?
- What is the delivery schedule?
- Which team members are assigned to which projects?
- Where are capacity risks?

**3D: Product/Innovation Priorities**
- Which products, experiments, or builds are planned this quarter?
- What is the resource allocation (hours)?
- What are the go/no-go criteria?

---

### Part 4: Commitment & Communication (30 min)

**4A: Priority Stack Ranking**
If we have to choose, what is the order of priority?
1. Agency revenue and client delivery (always #1)
2. MRR and retention
3. Product and innovation initiatives
4. Internal systems and infrastructure

**4B: What We're NOT Doing**
Explicitly naming what we are deferring this quarter to protect focus.

**4C: Communication Plan**
- What does the full team need to know about this quarter's priorities?
- How is this communicated (all-hands, Slack, written document)?

## Mid-Quarter Check-In (Month 2 of Quarter)

**Duration:** 60 minutes
**Participants:** CEO + department heads

**Agenda:**
1. OKR status: RAG (Red/Amber/Green) for each KR (15 min)
2. Red KRs: root cause + corrective action (30 min)
3. Any capacity or priority shifts needed? (15 min)

**Output:**
- Updated OKR status in Notion
- Escalation log for anything needing CEO decision
- Any OKR adjustments (rare — only for truly external changes)

## Quarterly Themes

Each quarter has a theme that helps the team remember the focus:

**Example Year 1:**
- Q1: "Build the Foundation" — systems, first clients, first content
- Q2: "Get to Revenue" — close first projects, launch first product
- Q3: "Build the Flywheel" — MRR, second wave of clients, community seed
- Q4: "Systematize Everything" — document, automate, plan Year 2

**How themes are used:**
- Named at start of quarter planning
- Referenced in team standup
- Used to filter decisions ("Does this serve our Q3 theme?")

## Quarter Wrap-Up Ritual

Last week of each quarter:

1. **OKR final grading** — all department heads submit grades with notes
2. **Team retro** — 60 min: what worked, what didn't, what to change
3. **Financial close** — CFO/CEO reviews quarter financials
4. **Client satisfaction pulse** — send NPS survey to active clients
5. **Celebration** — recognize accomplishments, shout out contributors
6. **Preview next quarter** — what's coming, build excitement
