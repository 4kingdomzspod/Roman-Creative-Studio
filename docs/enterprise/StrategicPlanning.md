## Purpose

Define how Roman Creative Studio sets long-range strategic direction — separating operational planning (what we do this quarter) from strategic planning (where we are going over the next 3–5 years).

**Business Value:** Most small agencies are trapped in reactive mode — responding to whatever client or problem arrives next. Strategic planning creates space to build the business deliberately. The agencies that scale are the ones that decide where they're going before the market decides for them.

---

## Strategic Planning vs. Operational Planning

| | Strategic Planning | Operational Planning |
|---|---|---|
| **Horizon** | 3–5 years | 90 days – 1 year |
| **Questions** | Where are we going? | How do we get there this quarter? |
| **Output** | Strategic themes, priorities, bets | OKRs, project plans, budgets |
| **Cadence** | Annual (with quarterly check) | Quarterly |
| **Owner** | CEO + leadership team | Department heads |
| **Participants** | Senior leadership | All managers |

## Strategic Framework: The Four Questions

Every strategic planning cycle answers these four questions:

**1. Where are we now?**
- Revenue, profitability, team health, client satisfaction, market position
- Honest assessment — no spin

**2. Where do we want to be in 3 years?**
- Revenue and profitability targets
- Market position and brand recognition
- Team size and structure
- Product portfolio
- Client profile

**3. What must be true for us to get there?**
- Strategic bets (the things that must go right)
- Capabilities we must develop
- Problems we must solve
- Risks we must manage

**4. What do we do first?**
- The 3–5 initiatives that will do the most to move us toward Year 3 vision
- These become the company's annual OKRs

## Strategic Planning Process

### Phase 1: Situation Analysis (October–November)

**1A: Financial Review**
- Revenue by stream (agency, products, courses, SaaS, community)
- Revenue vs. prior year
- MRR trend (12 months)
- Gross margin by service line
- Net profit margin
- Cash position and runway
- Owner compensation vs. plan

**1B: Market & Competitive Analysis**
- Which client industries grew or shrank for us?
- Which services are being requested more/less?
- Competitor moves: new offerings, pricing changes, talent
- Technology shifts affecting our market
- AI impact on our service delivery model

**1C: Customer Analysis**
- Client satisfaction scores (NPS, project scores)
- Retention rate: % of clients who stayed or expanded
- LTV by client tier and industry
- Lost clients: why did they leave?
- Best clients: what do they have in common?

**1D: Team Analysis**
- Headcount vs. plan
- Skills gaps identified this year
- Employee/contractor satisfaction
- Turnover and why
- Capacity: were we over/under capacity?

**1E: Product & Innovation Analysis**
- Products launched vs. planned
- Product revenue vs. target
- Experiments run: pass/fail rate
- Technology decisions made: good calls vs. regrets?

**1F: SWOT Analysis**
For each: be specific and evidence-based (not generic)

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|------------|
| What we do better than anyone | Where we consistently fall short |
| Our defensible advantages | Skills or capabilities we lack |
| What clients praise most | What clients complain about |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---------------|----------|
| Market shifts we can exploit | Competitive moves that could hurt us |
| Capabilities we could develop | Technology that could make us obsolete |
| Client segments we could enter | Economic conditions affecting clients |

---

### Phase 2: Strategic Choices (November)

**2A: Validate or Update the Vision**
- Is our 3-year vision still the right destination?
- What has changed that might require adjustment?
- Any markets or opportunities to add?
- Any to remove?

**2B: Identify Strategic Themes**
3–5 big themes that define the year's strategic direction.

Examples:
- Theme 1: "Deepen recurring revenue" — build Care Plan retention systems
- Theme 2: "Launch the education arm" — first 2 courses live by Q3
- Theme 3: "Validate SaaS opportunity" — 2 experiments with go/no-go criteria
- Theme 4: "Build the talent foundation" — first full-time hire, training systems

**2C: Strategic Bets**
For each theme, name the specific bet:
- What are we betting on?
- What would have to be true for this to succeed?
- What would cause this to fail?
- What is the reversibility? (can we undo this if wrong?)

**2D: Resource Allocation**
- Of our total capacity (CEO time + team time + budget), how much goes to each theme?
- Are we concentrating enough on priorities 1 and 2?
- Are we spreading too thin across 6+ initiatives?

---

### Phase 3: Strategic Plan Output (December)

**The Strategic Plan Document contains:**
1. Situation Summary (1 page): Where we are
2. 3-Year Vision (1 page): Where we're going
3. Strategic Themes (1 page): The 3–5 bets for the year
4. Annual Priorities (1 page): Top 5 initiatives this year
5. Resource Allocation (1 page): How we deploy time and money
6. Risk Register (1 page): Top risks and mitigations

**Length:** 6–10 pages maximum. Strategy documents longer than 10 pages are not read.

## Strategic Planning Retreat

**Cadence:** Annual (December, 2-day offsite or dedicated time)

**Participants:**
- Stage 1: CEO alone + 1 trusted advisor
- Stage 2: CEO + 1–2 key team members
- Stage 3+: Full leadership team

**Day 1: Reflection & Analysis**
- Morning: Financial and market review (Phase 1 data presented)
- Afternoon: SWOT + strategic themes discussion

**Day 2: Direction & Commitment**
- Morning: Validate vision, define strategic themes
- Afternoon: Annual priorities, resource allocation, OKR framing
- Evening: Commit and celebrate (team dinner)

**Pre-work (2 weeks before retreat):**
- Each department head prepares their domain analysis (1 page)
- Financial analysis compiled and distributed
- Prior year OKR results reviewed

## Strategic Priorities by Stage

### Stage 1 Priorities (Year 1)
1. Generate first $60k in agency revenue
2. Establish first MRR stream ($500+/mo)
3. Launch first digital product
4. Build operational foundation (systems, tools, processes)
5. Develop brand authority (content, positioning, portfolio)

### Stage 2 Priorities (Year 2–3)
1. Hire first team member (Creative Director or Account Manager)
2. Reach $10k/mo consistent agency revenue
3. Launch education arm (2 courses)
4. Launch community (Studio Membership)
5. Validate first SaaS opportunity
6. Build recurring revenue to 30%+ of total

### Stage 3 Priorities (Year 3–5)
1. Build leadership team (COO, CTO, CMO)
2. Launch first SaaS product
3. Reach $100k/mo total revenue
4. Enterprise client capability
5. National brand recognition in 2–3 verticals
6. Recurring revenue to 60%+ of total
