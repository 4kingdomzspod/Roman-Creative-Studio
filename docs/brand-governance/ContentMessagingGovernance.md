# Content & Messaging Governance
**Roman Creative Studio — Brand Governance System**
_Last Updated: 2025 | Version 1.0_

---

## Purpose

Define how Roman Creative Studio communicates in writing across every channel. Content governance ensures that every word — on the website, in a proposal, in a social caption, in an email — reflects the same brand, the same voice, and the same level of quality as the visual identity.

Inconsistent messaging is as damaging as inconsistent design. Both say: "We are not paying attention."

---

## 1. Tone Consistency Rules

The RCS tone system defines 7 modes (see `docs/brand/ToneOfVoice.md`). This document governs how those tones are applied consistently.

### Tone Application by Channel

| Channel | Primary Tone | Never Use |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Website homepage | Professional + Encouraging | Casual / Humorous |
| Service pages | Professional + Educational | Casual / Humorous |
| Blog / content | Educational + Friendly | Sales / Aggressive |
| Email (prospect) | Professional + Encouraging | Casual |
| Email (client) | Friendly + Professional | Stiff / Overly formal |
| Proposals | Professional + Educational | Casual / Conversational |
| Contracts | Professional | Any other mode |
| Social (LinkedIn) | Professional + Educational | Casual / Humorous |
| Social (Instagram) | Friendly + Encouraging | Stiff / Corporate |
| Social (TikTok) | Friendly + Educational | Stiff / Corporate |
| Error messages / 404 | Friendly + Encouraging | Apology-heavy / Technical |
| Button labels / CTAs | Direct / Action-oriented | Vague / Passive |

### Tone Drift Warning Signs

If you notice any of the following in content, flag for revision:
- Overly apologetic language ("We're so sorry, but...")
- Boastful or arrogant language ("The best agency in the world")
- Vague, non-committal phrasing ("We try to...")
- Excessive exclamation points (more than 1 per paragraph)
- Jargon that the client wouldn't use naturally
- Generic filler phrases (see Word List below)

## 2. Word Choice Guidelines

### Words & Phrases to Avoid

The following language is generic, overused in the agency industry, or misrepresents the RCS brand. It is prohibited in all client-facing and public content.

**Generic agency buzzwords:**
- ❌ "Cutting-edge"
- ❌ "Best-in-class"
- ❌ "World-class"
- ❌ "Innovative solutions"
- ❌ "Leverage" (as a verb for "use")
- ❌ "Synergy"
- ❌ "Ecosystem" (unless specifically referring to a technology ecosystem)
- ❌ "Disruptive" / "Disrupt"
- ❌ "Game-changer"
- ❌ "Holistic approach"
- ❌ "Seamless experience"
- ❌ "Robust"
- ❌ "Scalable solutions"
- ❌ "Move the needle"
- ❌ "Circle back"
- ❌ "Touch base"
- ❌ "Deep dive"
- ❌ "Low-hanging fruit"
- ❌ "Bandwidth" (unless referring to network bandwidth)
- ❌ "Ideate"
- ❌ "Pivot" (overused)

**Vague/empty claims:**
- ❌ "We are passionate about..."
- ❌ "We're dedicated to..."
- ❌ "We care about..."
- ❌ "The best [service] around"
- ❌ "Unlike other agencies" (without specifics)
- ❌ "We do things differently" (without specifics)

**Prohibited self-description without evidence:**
- ❌ "Award-winning" (unless true and documented)
- ❌ "Industry-leading" (unless substantiated)
- ❌ "Trusted by hundreds" (unless accurate)

### Approved Language Principles

In place of the above, use:
- Specific outcomes ("42% increase in organic traffic")
- Concrete processes ("We start every project with a strategy session")
- Direct benefit statements ("You get a site that loads in under 2 seconds")
- Honest differentiation ("We specialize in [specific niche] — it's all we do")
- Client language (use the words your clients use to describe their problems)

## 3. Messaging Hierarchy Rules

Every piece of content has a primary job. Before writing, identify:

1. **What is the single most important thing this content must communicate?**
2. **What is the one action the reader should take after reading this?**
3. **What objection does this content need to preemptively address?**

### Hierarchy Levels

| Level | Message Type | Example |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| L1 | Primary message | "We build websites that grow your business." |
| L2 | Supporting proof | "Every project includes strategy, design, and SEO." |
| L3 | Value proposition | "You get a senior team, not a junior freelancer." |
| L4 | Differentiator | "We specialize in [industry] — we know your buyers." |
| L5 | Trust signal | "We've built [X] websites. Here's one like yours." |
| L6 | CTA | "Schedule a free discovery call." |

**Rule:** Never lead with L3–L6. Lead with L1. The primary message comes first, always.

**Rule:** One primary message per page, per section, per email. Do not make the reader figure out what matters most.

## 4. CTA Consistency Rules

### Primary CTA — Site-Wide Standard

The primary CTA across the RCS website is:
> **"Book a Free Discovery Call"** or **"Schedule a Call"**

This CTA:
- Appears in the nav (always)
- Appears at the end of every service page
- Appears in the hero section
- Is always rendered as a Primary button (gold fill)

### Secondary CTA Library

The following CTAs are approved for secondary use. Use them consistently — do not invent new CTA copy without a documented reason.

| CTA Copy | Context |
|----------|---------|
| "View Our Work" | Portfolio / Case Studies section |
| "See Pricing" | Service pages, FAQ |
| "Learn More" | Section intros linking to subpages |
| "Read the Case Study" | Case study cards |
| "Download the Guide" | Lead magnet offers |
| "Get a Quote" | Pricing inquiries |
| "Talk to Us" | Informal contact prompts |
| "Start Your Project" | Project kickoff messaging |
| "See How It Works" | Process section |

### CTA Rules

- ❌ Never use vague CTAs: "Click Here", "Submit", "Go", "Learn"
- ❌ Never use more than one Primary CTA per section
- ❌ Never use passive CTA language: "You may want to contact us"
- ✅ CTAs must be action verbs: "Book", "Schedule", "View", "Get", "Start", "Download"
- ✅ CTAs state what the user gets, not what they do: "Get Your Free Proposal" not "Fill Out Form"

## 5. SEO Content Alignment Rules

Content that is well-written but not findable is wasted investment. All SEO-facing content must:

### Keyword Integration Rules

- **Primary keyword:** Used in page `<title>`, `<h1>`, first paragraph, and meta description
- **Secondary keywords:** Used naturally in `<h2>` and `<h3>` headings where appropriate
- **No keyword stuffing.** If a sentence sounds unnatural, rewrite it—don't force the keyword
- **One primary keyword per page.** Not one per section — one per page

### Content Length Standards

| Page Type | Minimum Word Count |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Homepage | 800 words |
| Service page | 1,200 words |
| Industry/location page | 1,000 words |
| Blog post | 1,500 words |
| Case study | 600 words |
| About page | 600 words |

**Quality rule:** These are minimums. More words are not better if they add nothing. Every sentence must earn its place.

### SEO Copy Rules

- **Title tags:** 50–60 characters, include primary keyword and brand name
- **Meta descriptions:** 140–160 characters, action-oriented, include a CTA phrase
- **H1:** Exactly one per page, primary keyword naturally included
- **Internal links:** Every page should link to at least 2 related internal pages
- **Alt text:** Every image must have descriptive alt text (see AccessibilityGovernance.md)

## 6. Messaging Consistency Framework

All RCS content — regardless of who writes it — must trace back to the core messaging pillars defined in `docs/brand/MessagingFramework.md`.

### The Core Message (Always True)

> **Roman Creative Studio helps businesses build trust, attract the right customers, and grow — through thoughtful strategy, premium design, and technology that works.**

Every piece of content should reinforce one or more of these three pillars:
1. **Build trust** — credibility, quality, social proof
2. **Attract right customers** — targeting, strategy, positioning
3. **Grow** — outcomes, results, ROI

### Pre-Publication Content Check

Before any content is published:

- [ ] Does it reinforce at least one of the three core message pillars?
- [ ] Is the primary message in the first paragraph?
- [ ] Does it use approved language (not prohibited buzzwords)?
- [ ] Is the tone correct for this channel?
- [ ] Does it have a clear, specific CTA?
- [ ] Has it been proofread for grammar and spelling?
- [ ] Does it avoid unsupported superlatives?
- [ ] Is the keyword strategy clear (for SEO content)?
- [ ] Does the length match the minimum standard for this content type?

| Case studies | Ongoing | New projects available |
