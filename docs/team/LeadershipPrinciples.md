# Leadership Principles
# Roman Creative Studio — Team & Leadership Operating System

## Purpose

Define the leadership principles that govern how leaders at Roman Creative Studio operate — how they make decisions, develop their teams, communicate, and drive the business forward. These apply to the CEO today and to every future manager, director, and executive.

**Business Value:** Leadership principles create consistency in management. A team member in any department should receive the same quality of leadership experience. These principles enable coaching, feedback, and leadership evaluation.

## Principle 1: Lead by Example

**"You cannot ask others to do what you will not do yourself."**

Leaders at Roman Creative Studio hold themselves to the same standards they hold their teams. If documentation is required, leaders document. If communication standards are set, leaders follow them. If quality is expected, leaders deliver quality work.

**In practice:**
- CEOs and directors review their own outputs before submitting
- Leaders arrive on time to meetings they set
- Leaders write clear, professional communications that model the standard
- Leaders complete training when new standards are introduced

---

## Principle 2: Develop People First

**"The quality of your team is the quality of your leadership."**

Leaders at RCS are coaches, not just managers. Their primary responsibility is developing the capability of their team. A leader who hoards knowledge, keeps people dependent, or fails to grow their team is failing at leadership.

**In practice:**
- Every direct report has a documented career development plan
- Leaders give specific, actionable feedback within 48 hours of work submission
- Leaders actively work to make themselves replaceable in their current role
- Leaders identify and remove barriers that slow their team's performance
- Leaders invest in their team's education budget before their own (at Stage 3+)

---

## Principle 3: Bias for Clarity

**"Confusion is a leadership failure. Clarity is your product."**

Unclear instructions, vague goals, and ambiguous expectations cost time and money. Leaders at RCS create clarity before they ask for execution. Every assignment includes: what, why, when, and what good looks like.

**In practice:**
- Every project brief answers: objective, audience, constraints, deadline, success criteria
- Leaders document decisions in writing after making them
- Leaders ask "did I make that clear?" not "did you understand?"
- When something is confusing, leaders improve the documentation, not just explain verbally

---

## Principle 4: Assume Positive Intent

**"Interpret ambiguous actions charitably until evidence says otherwise."**

When a teammate misses a deadline, submits subpar work, or communicates poorly, the default assumption is that something got in the way — not that they're lazy or don't care. This assumption enables honest conversations without defensiveness.

**In practice:**
- Leaders ask "what happened?" before "why didn't you?"
- Leaders investigate context before assigning blame
- Leaders distinguish between a pattern and an incident
- Leaders address issues directly but without assuming malice

---

## Principle 5: Decide and Move

**"Slow decisions cost as much as wrong decisions. Choose, execute, adjust."**

Perfect information never arrives. Leaders at RCS make timely decisions with available information, communicate the decision clearly, and adjust when new data arrives. Analysis paralysis is a failure mode.

**In practice:**
- Decisions with <$500 impact: make independently and document
- Decisions with $500–$5,000 impact: consult 1 other stakeholder, then decide
- Decisions with >$5,000 impact: structured discussion with CEO and relevant parties
- After a decision: document it, communicate it, move
- If a decision proves wrong: acknowledge, course-correct, don't relitigate

---

## Principle 6: Protect the Team's Focus

**"A distracted team is an unproductive team. Leaders absorb chaos."**

When the business faces uncertainty, complexity, or pressure, leaders shield their team from unnecessary noise so they can stay focused on excellent work. Leaders handle problems up and out — not down.

**In practice:**
- Leaders don't share every business stress with the entire team
- Leaders resolve client escalations before they become team disruptions
- Leaders create structures (meetings, async channels) that reduce interruptions
- Leaders set clear priorities so teams don't face constant context-switching

---

## Principle 7: Give and Receive Feedback Continuously

**"The annual review should never contain surprises."**

Feedback is a real-time practice at RCS — not a calendar event. Leaders give specific feedback on specific work, as close to the moment as possible. They also seek feedback on their own leadership performance.

**Feedback framework (SBI model):**
```
Situation: "In yesterday's client call..."
Behavior: "...you interrupted the client twice before they finished."
Impact: "This made them defensive and we had to spend 10 minutes recovering the relationship."
```

**In practice:**
- Leaders give written feedback within 48 hours of project deliverable
- Leaders ask for feedback on their management at every 90-day review
- Leaders conduct 1:1s every week with direct reports (not monthly)
- Leaders never save critical feedback for the annual review

---

## Principle 8: Measure What Matters

**"If you can't measure it, you can't manage it. If you measure too much, you measure nothing."**

Leaders at RCS identify the 3–5 metrics that genuinely indicate team and business health. They track them consistently, share them transparently, and act on them when they deviate.

**In practice:**
- Every department has 3–5 KPIs reviewed monthly
- Leaders share KPI status with their team, not just upward
- Leaders distinguish between lagging indicators (revenue) and leading indicators (leads, pipeline)
- Leaders don't add metrics without removing others

---

## Principle 9: Build Systems, Not Dependencies

**"If the business only works when you're there, you haven't built a business."**

Leaders document their work so others can execute it. They build processes that survive personnel changes. They resist the ego-driven trap of being indispensable.

**In practice:**
- Every recurring task leaders perform has a documented SOP
- Leaders train their backup before they're needed
- Leaders actively reduce single points of failure in their departments
- Leaders celebrate when a teammate executes something independently for the first time

---

## Principle 10: Be Honest About Hard Things

**"Uncomfortable truth today prevents catastrophe tomorrow."**

Leaders at RCS communicate bad news early and directly. They don't sugarcoat poor performance, miss revenue projections without acknowledgment, or hide client relationship problems. Honesty builds trust; avoidance destroys it.

**In practice:**
- Leaders tell clients about delays before deadlines pass
- Leaders tell the CEO about business problems before they become crises
- Leaders deliver termination, demotion, or negative performance news clearly and humanely
- Leaders acknowledge their own mistakes before their team points them out

---

## Leadership Evaluation (Annual)

Leaders at Stage 3+ are evaluated on their leadership behaviors, not just business outcomes. This evaluation is done:
- By their direct reports (360-degree feedback)
- By their peers (cross-department)
- By the CEO (for department directors)

**Leadership score components:**
1. Team development (did their team grow?)
2. Clarity and communication (did the team always know what was expected?)
3. Decision quality (were decisions made timely and wisely?)
4. Feedback culture (did they give and receive feedback well?)
5. System-building (did they reduce dependencies?)
6. Values alignment (did they model company values?)

**Minimum score to remain in leadership role:** 3.5 / 5.0
