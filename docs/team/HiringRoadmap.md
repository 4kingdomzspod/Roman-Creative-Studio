## Purpose

Define exactly when, why, and how Roman Creative Studio hires each new team member — from first contractor to full agency team. Every hire is triggered by measurable business conditions, not gut feel.

**Business Value:** Prevents premature hiring (cash drain) and late hiring (quality breakdown). Creates a clear growth path from solo to team.

**Owner:** CEO  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Related Documents:** OrganizationChart.md, RoleDescriptions.md, FinancialKPIs.md, MRRArchitecture.md

---

## Hiring Philosophy

1. **Revenue before headcount.** Never hire before revenue supports it. MRR first, then payroll.
2. **Contractors before full-time.** Validate need with contract/freelance before committing to salary.
3. **Document before you delegate.** Every role must have a written job description and documented processes before first day.
4. **Culture fit is non-negotiable.** Skills can be trained. Values and work ethic cannot.
5. **Slow to hire, quick to address.** Take time selecting the right person. But address performance issues immediately.

## Hiring Sequence

### HIRE-01: First Contractor — Copywriter

**Timing:** Month 3–6 (when first project closes)  
**Trigger:** CEO spending >4 hours/project on copywriting OR client requests dedicated copy help  
**Type:** Freelance / Project-based  
**Budget:** $200–$500/project  
**Scope:** Website copywriting, case studies, email sequences  
**Source:** Referral, Contra, LinkedIn, Copywriter portfolio sites  

**Why first:** Copy is high-impact but not technically specialized. Freeing CEO copy time directly increases project capacity.

---

### HIRE-02: Second Contractor — Frontend Developer (Overflow)

**Timing:** Month 4–8 (when 2+ projects active simultaneously)  
**Trigger:** 2 active projects at once OR CEO working weekends to meet deadlines  
**Type:** Freelance / Hourly  
**Budget:** $50–$75/hour, 10–20 hours/project  
**Scope:** Development implementation (HTML/CSS/JS) from Figma designs  
**Source:** GitHub, LinkedIn, developer communities, referral  

**Why second:** Development is time-intensive. Outsourcing implementation frees CEO for design, sales, and client management.

---

### HIRE-03: Part-Time Designer or Project Coordinator

**Timing:** Month 8–14 (when MRR hits $2,000+)  
**Trigger:** MRR ≥ $2,000/month AND CEO regularly exceeding 50 hours/week  
**Type:** Part-time employee (20 hr/week) or PT contractor  
**Compensation:** $20–$28/hour ($1,600–$2,240/month)  
**Role Options:**
  - **Designer:** Takes UI implementation from Figma mockup to development-ready specs. Requires Figma proficiency.
  - **Project Coordinator:** Manages timelines, client emails, milestone tracking. Requires organization + communication.

**Decision logic:** If CEO bottleneck is creative → hire Designer. If CEO bottleneck is communication/admin → hire Coordinator.

---

### HIRE-04: First Full-Time Employee

**Timing:** Year 2 (Month 12–18, when MRR hits $3,500+)  
**Trigger:**
  - MRR ≥ $3,500/month (covers salary + benefits)
  - Project revenue $8,000+/month average over 3 consecutive months
  - CEO capacity maxed for 60+ consecutive days

**Role:** Frontend Developer (Full-Time)  
**Compensation:** $55,000–$70,000/year ($4,583–$5,833/month)  
**Why Developer:** Development is the most time-intensive service. A full-time developer unlocks capacity for 2–3x more projects.

**Benefits at this stage:** Health insurance stipend ($300/month), 15 days PTO, flexible hours, remote work

---

### HIRE-05: Creative Director (or Senior Designer)

**Timing:** Year 2–3 (MRR $5,000+)  
**Trigger:**
  - Design quality bottleneck affecting project delivery
  - CEO can no longer maintain design quality while doing CEO work
  - 3+ active projects requiring design simultaneously

**Role:** Creative Director or Senior UI Designer  
**Compensation:** $65,000–$85,000/year  
**Why:** Creative quality is the #1 brand differentiator. A Creative Director protects and elevates that standard while freeing CEO for growth.

---

### HIRE-06: Sales or Marketing Manager

**Timing:** Year 3 (MRR $7,000+, revenue $20,000+/month)  
**Trigger:**
  - Inbound leads consistently arriving but CEO too busy to close them
  - Marketing output (blog, social, podcast) degraded due to CEO capacity
  - Pipeline conversion <35% due to slow follow-up

**Role:** Marketing Manager or Sales Manager (choose based on biggest gap)  
**Compensation:** $50,000–$65,000/year + performance bonus  
**Why:** Revenue growth stalls when the CEO is the only person doing sales AND delivery. This hire unlocks the next revenue ceiling.

---

### HIRE-07: Operations Manager

**Timing:** Year 3–4 (team size 4+)  
**Trigger:**
  - CEO spending >10 hours/week on operations, scheduling, and internal coordination
  - Project delivery consistency dropping below 85%
  - Team communication breaking down (missed handoffs, unclear ownership)

**Role:** Operations Manager  
**Compensation:** $55,000–$70,000/year  
**Why:** Without an Operations Manager, the CEO becomes the operational bottleneck. This hire enables CEO to focus on vision, clients, and growth.

---

### HIRE-08: Account Manager / Customer Success

**Timing:** Year 3–4 (20+ active care plan clients)  
**Trigger:**
  - Care plan client count exceeds 20
  - Monthly reports being delivered late or skipped
  - Client satisfaction trending down
  - Churn rate rising above 5%/month

**Role:** Account Manager / Customer Success Specialist  
**Compensation:** $45,000–$60,000/year  
**Why:** At 20+ care clients, delivery quality without dedicated account management degrades. Retention is cheaper than acquisition.

## Contractor vs. Employee Decision Framework

| Factor | Contractor | Full-Time Employee |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Need is project-based | ✓ | ✗ |
| Need is ongoing and predictable | ✗ | ✓ |
| Revenue stable enough for salary | ✗ (not yet) | ✓ |
| You want IP ownership | ✓ (with contract) | ✓ |
| Need behavioral integration with culture | ✗ | ✓ |
| Specialized skill you can't train | ✓ | Depends |
| Budget <$4,000/month for role | ✓ (often) | ✗ |
| Need is 20+ hours/week consistently | Consider FT | ✓ |

## Freelancer Pool Strategy

Maintain a vetted pool of 5–10 freelancers across disciplines:
- 2 copywriters
- 2 frontend developers
- 1 UI designer
- 1 SEO specialist
- 1 photographer
- 1 video editor (Year 2+)
- 1 brand/print designer

**Vetting process:**
1. Portfolio review
2. Paid test project ($100–$200)
3. NDA and contractor agreement signed
4. W-9 collected before first payment
5. First project with close oversight
6. Quality assessment after first project (keep or cull)

## Internship Program (Year 3+)

**Available when:** Team size ≥ 4, dedicated mentor available (Creative Director or Engineering Lead)

**Internship types:**
- Design Intern (6 months, part-time) — assists UI/UX team
- Development Intern (6 months, part-time) — assists Engineering team
- Marketing Intern (3–6 months, part-time) — content and SEO support

**Compensation:** $15–$18/hour

**Goal:** Pipeline for entry-level hires. Best interns receive full-time offer.

**Requirements:**
- Documented intern project with learning objectives
- Weekly 1:1 with mentor
- Mid-internship and final performance review
- Structured learning curriculum from Training.md

## Hiring Process (Standard)

1. **Define the role** — job description from RoleDescriptions.md, finalize before posting
2. **Source candidates** — LinkedIn, Contra, referrals, Indeed, portfolio sites
3. **Application review** — portfolio/resume screen (CEO or Creative Director)
4. **First interview** — 30-minute video call (culture, background, motivations)
5. **Skills test** — paid test project ($100–$300 depending on role)
6. **Final interview** — 60-minute deep dive with CEO and relevant team member
7. **Reference check** — 2 professional references
8. **Offer** — written offer letter with compensation, start date, and expectations
9. **Onboarding** — follow Onboarding.md protocol

**Time-to-hire target:** 3–5 weeks from posting to start date

## Compensation Philosophy

- **Pay competitively within the market for the role.** Use Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, and AngelList data.
- **Tie variable pay to performance.** Base salary + performance bonus (10–15% of base at target).
- **Increase compensation annually.** Minimum cost-of-living (3–5%) + merit increases for strong performers.
- **Benefits matter.** Remote flexibility, PTO, education budget, and health stipend attract quality talent at Stage 2–3 even when salary can't compete with large agencies.

**Benefits progression:**

| Stage | Benefits Offered |
|-------|----------------|
| Contractor | Rate-only |
| Part-time | Flexible hours + remote |
| First FT hire | 15 PTO days + $300/mo health stipend + $500/yr education |
| Stage 3 team | Above + $1,000/yr conference + formal performance bonuses |
| Stage 4+ | Above + 401k match (3%) + expanded benefits |
