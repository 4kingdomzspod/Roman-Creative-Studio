# Roman Creative Studio — Outreach Playbook

This is the working guide for finding, contacting, and converting new clients. It covers the daily workflow, how to qualify a lead, the follow-up cadence, what to do on a discovery call, and how a proposal gets from "yes, let's talk" to a signed project.

Pair this with the templates in this folder:
- `cold-email.md`
- `instagram-dm.md`
- `follow-up-1.md`
- `follow-up-2.md`
- `phone-script.md`
- `lead-tracker.csv`

**Core rule across every piece of outreach:** don't say anything that isn't true. No invented client counts, no fabricated results or percentages, no guarantees. What we can honestly say: we build custom, mobile-first websites focused on lead generation and local SEO, we have two completed client projects to show (Iconic Rentals and Bouquetsscl), the first call is free, and we typically send a proposal within 24 hours of a discovery call with most projects live in 4–6 weeks. Lead with that — it's enough.

---

## 1. Daily Workflow

A simple loop, done consistently, beats a big push once a month.

**Each outreach day:**

1. **Review `lead-tracker.csv` first.** Check anything due for follow-up today (see cadence below) before finding new leads — warm follow-ups matter more than cold volume.
2. **Send due follow-ups** using `follow-up-1.md` or `follow-up-2.md` as appropriate. Update Status and Follow-up date immediately after sending.
3. **Prospect new leads.** Find local service businesses that fit (see Qualification below), and add them to `lead-tracker.csv` with Source and Status set to `New`.
4. **Send new outreach** (cold email, Instagram DM, or a call) to a batch of qualified new leads. Log First Contact date and update Status to `Contacted`.
5. **Log every reply the same day it comes in** — update Status, add Notes, and schedule the next action (call, follow-up, or close).
6. **End of day:** scan the tracker for anything that slipped — no lead should sit untouched past its follow-up date.

Keep the daily volume sustainable. A smaller number of personalized, well-researched outbound messages will outperform a large batch of generic ones — and every template in this folder depends on being personalized before it's sent.

## 2. Qualification Process

Not every local business is a fit right now. Before spending time on outreach, check:

**Good-fit signals:**
- Local service business (the site currently serves dentists and churches directly, with messaging built to extend to other local service businesses — home services, restaurants, and similar).
- No website, or a website that's clearly outdated, not mobile-friendly, or missing a clear way to book/call/contact.
- Business appears active (recent posts, current hours, real reviews on Google/Facebook) — a website investment only makes sense for a business that's actually running.
- A decision-maker is identifiable (owner, office manager, marketing contact) — avoid businesses where you can't tell who'd make this call.

**Lower-priority or skip:**
- Business already has a modern, well-functioning site with clear booking/contact flows — there's no obvious gap to open a conversation around.
- Business appears inactive or closed.
- No way to identify or reach a decision-maker.

**Qualifying questions to ask early** (phone or first reply):
- Do you currently have a website?
- How's it working for bringing in new customers?
- Are you the right person to talk to about this?
- Is this the first time a new site has come up, or have you been actively looking?

A lead is "qualified" once you can answer: *do they have a real gap, and can they say yes if they want to move forward?* If not, keep them in the tracker as `Nurture` and revisit later rather than pushing now.

## 3. Follow-Up Cadence

No response doesn't mean no — most replies come after a follow-up, not the first message. Stick to this cadence and stop after the second follow-up if there's still nothing.

| Step | Timing | Template | Status after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial contact | Day 0 | `cold-email.md` or `instagram-dm.md` or `phone-script.md` | `Contacted` |
| Follow-up 1 | Day 3–4 (business days) | `follow-up-1.md` | `Follow-up 1 Sent` |
| Follow-up 2 (final) | Day 10–14 (business days) | `follow-up-2.md` | `Follow-up 2 Sent` |
| No response after Follow-up 2 | — | — | `No Response` — stop outreach unless they reply |

**If they reply at any point** (interested, not interested, or "not right now"):
- Interested → move to scheduling a discovery call, Status `Call Booked`.
- Not interested → thank them, respect it, Status `Closed — Not Interested`. Don't argue or push.
- Not right now → ask permission to check back in a few months, Status `Nurture`, set a Follow-up date accordingly.

Never send a third unsolicited follow-up. If someone explicitly asks not to be contacted, mark `Do Not Contact` and stop immediately.

## 4. Discovery Call Checklist

Use this for every booked call — before, during, and after.

**Before the call:**
- [ ] Look at the business's current website (if any) and note 2–3 specific, honest observations.
- [ ] Check their Google Business Profile / social presence for context (industry, location, how established they are).
- [ ] Confirm the lead's info in `lead-tracker.csv` is up to date (contact name, email, phone).
- [ ] Have `pricing.html` tiers in mind ($3,500 / $6,500 / $12k+) so you can speak to scope naturally if asked.

**During the call (aim for ~30 minutes):**
- [ ] Introduce Roman Creative Studio briefly — what we do and how we work.
- [ ] Ask about their business: goals, current site situation, what's not working.
- [ ] Ask about timeline and decision process (who else is involved, when do they want to launch).
- [ ] Share relevant examples from `romancreativestudio.co/portfolio` if useful.
- [ ] Give an honest read on scope and which pricing tier likely fits — don't over-promise on price or timeline.
- [ ] Confirm next step: proposal within 24 hours if it's a fit.

**After the call:**
- [ ] Log call notes in `lead-tracker.csv` the same day — goals, budget signals, timeline, objections raised.
- [ ] Update Status to `Proposal Pending` if moving forward, or the appropriate closed/nurture status if not.
- [ ] Start the proposal (see below) immediately — the 24-hour turnaround starts now.

---

## 5. Proposal Workflow

1. **Draft the proposal within 24 hours of the discovery call.** This is a real commitment already made on the site (`process.html`) — don't let it slip.
2. **Base scope and price on what was actually discussed** — page count, features (booking, giving, integrations), and which pricing tier fits. Don't quote something that wasn't grounded in the call.
3. **Include:** scope of work, timeline (typically 4–6 weeks from kickoff), investment amount, payment schedule, and what's needed from the client to get started (brand assets, content, sign-off points).
4. **Send the proposal by email**, referencing the call directly so it doesn't feel generic.
5. **Log it:** update `lead-tracker.csv` Status to `Proposal Sent`, with the date in Notes.
6. **Follow up if there's no response** within about a week — a short, direct check-in is fine here; don't let a sent proposal go silent without a nudge.
7. **On acceptance:** move Status to `Won` and hand off to the project kickoff process (see `process.html` for the build workflow — discovery, strategy, design, development, launch).
8. **On decline or no response after following up:** move Status to `Closed — Lost`, and note the reason in Notes if known (helps spot patterns over time).

## Status Values Reference (for `lead-tracker.csv`)

Use these consistently so the tracker stays useful:

`New` → `Contacted` → `Follow-up 1 Sent` → `Follow-up 2 Sent` → `No Response`
(or, once they engage) → `Call Booked` → `Proposal Pending` → `Proposal Sent` → `Won` / `Closed — Lost`

Other statuses: `Nurture` (not now, check back later), `Closed — Not Interested`, `Do Not Contact`.
