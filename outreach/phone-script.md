# Cold Call / Discovery Call Phone Script

This is a guide, not a word-for-word script. Use it to stay on track and make sure nothing important gets missed — talk like a person, not a script-reader.

**No invented stats, no promised results, no "guaranteed" language, at any point in the call.**

---

## 1. Opener (first 10–15 seconds)

> "Hi, is this {{First Name}}? Hey, this is {{Your Name}} with Roman Creative Studio — I know this is a cold call, so I'll keep it quick. Do you have 60 seconds, or is now a bad time?"

- If it's a bad time: ask when's better, and confirm a callback time before hanging up.
- If they say yes: move to the hook.

## 2. The hook (why you're calling)

> "I build custom websites for local service businesses — the short version is I help make sure your site actually turns visitors into calls and bookings, not just a nicer-looking homepage. I noticed {{specific, true observation about their current site or online presence}}, and wanted to reach out directly instead of just emailing."

## 3. Permission-based qualifying questions

Ask, don't interrogate. The goal is to understand if there's a real fit before pitching anything.

- "Do you currently have a website, or are you working without one right now?"
- "How's it working for you as far as bringing in new customers?"
- "Are you the right person to talk to about this, or is that someone else on your team?"
- "Is a new site or a redesign something you've thought about, or is this the first time it's come up?"

## 4. Value proposition (only after you've listened)

> "What we do is build custom sites — mobile-first, built around local SEO, with a clear path for someone to actually book or call you. First step, if it makes sense, is a free 30-minute call where we look at what you've got and figure out if we're a good fit. If we move forward, we usually send a full proposal within 24 hours of that call, and most projects go live in about 4 to 6 weeks."

If they ask for examples:
> "Sure — you can see two completed client projects at romancreativestudio.co/portfolio. One's a Miami yacht and car rental company, the other's a handmade bouquet business."

If they ask about pricing:
> "Projects start at $3,500 for a smaller site and scale up from there depending on scope — happy to get specific once we know what you need. There's no cost or obligation for the first call."

## 5. Common objections

**"We already have a website."**
> "Totally fair — a lot of the people I talk to do. Would you be open to me taking a quick look and sharing one or two honest thoughts? No pitch, just a second opinion."

**"We're not looking to spend money on this right now."**
> "Understood — no pressure at all. Would it be okay if I checked back in a few months, or would you rather I not follow up?"

**"How much does this cost?"**
> "Projects start at $3,500 and scale based on scope — pages, features, integrations. The first call is free either way, and that's usually where we figure out what makes sense for you specifically."

**"Send me some information instead."**
> "Happy to — what's the best email? I'll send over our portfolio and pricing so you can look at it on your own time."

**"I'm not interested."**
> "No problem at all, thanks for your time — have a good one."
(Do not push further. Log the call and move on.)

## 6. The close

If there's genuine interest:
> "Great — let's grab 30 minutes on the calendar. I'll send you a link right now: romancreativestudio.co/book.html — what's the best email to send that to?"

If they're not ready but not a hard no:
> "No problem — mind if I follow up by email with a bit more info, and you can reach out whenever it makes sense?"

## 7. After the call

- Log the call outcome in `lead-tracker.csv` immediately (Status, First Contact, Notes).
- If a call was booked, confirm the discovery call checklist in `OUTREACH_PLAYBOOK.md` is ready before that call happens.
- If they asked for info by email, send it the same day.
