# Sales Messaging System
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 5, Document 12

---

### Messaging Philosophy

Every word in every sales interaction communicates something about Roman Creative Studio. The way RCS writes an email, opens a call, or responds to a LinkedIn message either reinforces the premium positioning or undermines it.

Premium agencies do not sound desperate. They do not beg for the business. They do not apologize for their pricing or over-explain their worth. They communicate with the confidence of someone who knows the value of what they deliver and respects the prospect enough to be direct.

**Three-word positioning in every communication:** Authority. Clarity. Respect.

---

## Section 1 — Tone Guidelines

### 1.1 What RCS Sounds Like

| Principle | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Authoritative** | Speaks from expertise, not from hope | "For a dental practice at your stage, here's what we'd prioritize" |
| **Direct** | Says what it means without burying the point | "The investment for this is $12,500." |
| **Confident** | Does not hedge or apologize for value | "This is what it takes to produce the result." |
| **Specific** | Uses real details, not generic claims | "We've built 12 dental practice websites in the last 2 years" |
| **Human** | Warm and genuine, not robotic | "I genuinely looked at your site before this call" |
| **Respectful** | Treats the prospect as a capable adult | "I'd rather be straight with you than waste your time" |

### 1.2 What RCS Does NOT Sound Like

| Anti-pattern | Why It Undermines Positioning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Desperate** | Signals low demand | "I'd love to work with you on anything you need" |
| **Apologetic** | Signals insecurity | "I know this might be a lot" |
| **Vague** | Signals lack of substance | "We're passionate about helping businesses grow" |
| **Pushy** | Creates resistance | "I just wanted to follow up again..." |
| **Salesy** | Triggers skepticism | "This is a limited-time offer" |
| **Sycophantic** | Signals desperation | "I would be so honored to work with your brand" |

## Section 2 — Words to Use

### 2.1 Value & Positioning Language

| Instead of... | Use... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "cost" | "investment" | Frames as business decision, not expense |
| "price" | "investment" | Same |
| "cheap" | "lower investment level" | Maintains dignity |
| "service" | "solution" | Solution implies a problem being solved |
| "we do" | "we build" / "we engineer" | More precise, signals expertise |
| "nice website" | "conversion-optimized digital presence" | Substance over aesthetics |
| "design" (alone) | "design and strategy" | Signals depth beyond visual work |
| "client" (alone) | "strategic partner" (in context) | Elevates the relationship |

### 2.2 Action & CTA Language

| Instead of... | Use... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Let me know if you're interested" | "Are you ready to move forward?" | Direct ask, creates decision |
| "Feel free to reach out" | "Book a call here: [link]" | Specific CTA |
| "We can discuss" | "Here's what I'd recommend" | Authority |
| "Hopefully we can work together" | "I think there's a strong case here" | Confidence |
| "If you have any questions" | "If anything's unclear" | Subtle framing shift |
| "Let me know what you think" | "What's your reaction?" | More direct |

### 2.3 Discovery & Diagnostic Language

```
Strong discovery phrases:
"What specifically is the website failing to do right now?"
"If you had to quantify what this is costing you — what would you say?"
"What would success look like in 90 days after launch?"
"What have you tried that hasn't worked?"
"Who's the decision-maker on this project?"
"Is there a hard deadline driving this?"
```

```
Positioning phrases during the call:
"Based on what you've described..."
"In our experience with [industry] businesses..."
"The reason most [industry] websites underperform is..."
"What we find consistently across this vertical is..."
"That's a pattern we see often — here's what it usually comes from."
```

## Section 3 — Words to Avoid

### 3.1 Absolute Prohibitions (from Phase 2C ContentMessagingGovernance)

These words and phrases are banned in all RCS communication:

```
cutting-edge        — overused, means nothing
innovative          — overused, means nothing
seamless            — means nothing, sounds like an ad
leveraging          — corporate jargon
synergy             — meaningless
passionate about    — every agency says this
world-class         — unsubstantiated
best-in-class       — unsubstantiated
one-stop shop       — signals generalist mediocrity
we take pride in    — weak self-description
holistic approach   — vague and overused
client-centric      — meaningless; every agency claims this
results-driven      — everyone claims it, no one proves it
```

### 3.2 Weak Sales Language to Avoid

```
"Just wanted to.."         — signals apologetic tone
"I hope this finds you"    — filler, skip it
"Sorry to bother you"      — never apologize for professional outreach
"If you're interested"     — weak conditional — assume they should be
"Of course, no pressure"   — said too often, signals desperation
"Whatever works for you"   — positions RCS as subordinate
"Any questions at all"     — filler, creates open-endedness without direction
"I was hoping"             — hope is not a business strategy
```

## Section 4 — Email Tone Structure

Every RCS sales email follows a consistent structure:

### 4.1 Email Architecture

```
OPENING:   Direct. No filler. Reference something specific.
BODY:      Maximum 3 short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
CTA:       One clear next step. Not three options.
CLOSING:   First name only. No "Best regards" or "Warm wishes."
```

### 4.2 Subject Line Rules

| Rule | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Specific | "Your website audit — [Business Name]" | "Checking in" |
| Short | Under 50 characters | "I wanted to follow up on our recent conversation about..." |
| No clickbait | "Website observation for [Name]" | "You won't believe what I found" |
| No all caps | "Quick note for [First Name]" | "IMPORTANT: Your Website Audit" |
| First name if known | "[First Name] — one thought on [Business]" | "Hello" |

### 4.3 Email Signature

```
Alexander
Roman Creative Studio
Alexander@romancreativestudio.co
romancreativestudio.co
```

No motivational quotes. No extensive credential lists. No "Sent from my iPhone."
Clean. Professional. Memorable.

## Section 5 — Phone / Video Call Tone

### 5.1 Opening

Never: "Hi! How are you? How's your day going?"
Always: Short warm acknowledgment, then immediately take control of the call's purpose.

```
"[First Name], good to connect. Here's what I want to cover today:
[clear agenda]. Does that work for you?"
```

### 5.2 During the Call

- Speak slower than feels natural. Confident people don't rush.
- Use the prospect's name occasionally — not constantly.
- When making a strong point: pause after it. Let it land.
- When they're talking: listen completely. Don't formulate your next sentence.
- Never interrupt. Not even to agree.

### 5.3 Pacing the Close

When presenting the investment:
- State the number at a normal pace
- Pause completely (2–3 seconds)
- Do not explain, justify, or add to the number
- Wait for their reaction

## Section 6 — CTA Language Rules

### 6.1 In Emails

One CTA per email. Specific action. Clear next step.

| Context | CTA |
|---|---|
| Cold outreach | "Would a 20-minute call make sense this week?" |
| Post-discovery | "I'll have the proposal to you by [day]." |
| Post-proposal | "Sign here when you're ready to proceed: [link]" |
| Follow-up #1 | "Any questions before you make your decision?" |
| Final follow-up | "Let me know if you're moving forward or need to pause for now." |

### 6.2 In Proposals

CTA at the end of every proposal:
```
"Ready to move forward? Here's what happens next:

  1. Sign the project agreement
  2. Submit the initial deposit
  3. We schedule your kickoff call within 48 hours

[Sign & Begin Button]"
```

### 6.3 On the Website

From Phase 4 CTA Strategy System:
- Primary: "Book a Free Discovery Call"
- Secondary: "View Our Work"
- Tertiary: email link at Alexander@romancreativestudio.co

These are consistent across all pages. No variation in primary CTA label.

## Section 7 — The Authority Test

Before sending any sales communication, run it through the Authority Test:

**Ask:**
1. Does this communicate confidence or desperation?
2. Is every word earning its place or is it filler?
3. Does the CTA give one clear next step?
4. Would a premium agency send this?
5. Would I be proud for a prospect to forward this to someone who doesn't know RCS?

If any answer is no: revise before sending.
