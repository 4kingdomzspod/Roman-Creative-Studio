# Client Qualification System
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 5, Document 4

---

### Why Qualification Matters More Than Volume

The most expensive thing RCS can do is take on the wrong client.

A bad-fit client costs:
- Time (scope creep, revision loops, difficult communication)
- Energy (emotional drain, creative compromise)
- Money (under-scoped, underpriced, or over-delivered)
- Reputation (unsatisfied clients become negative word-of-mouth)
- Opportunity cost (time that could have been spent on ideal clients)

A strict qualification system protects all of the above. It is not gatekeeping — it is professionalism.

---

## Section 1 — The Five Qualification Dimensions

Every prospect is evaluated across five dimensions before a proposal is prepared:

### Dimension 1: Budget Alignment

**Question:** Does this prospect understand and accept that premium work has premium investment?

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Has stated a real budget range that aligns with RCS minimums | +2 |
| Has not mentioned budget but hasn't objected to price anchors | +1 |
| Has asked “how much?” as first question but is still engaged | 0 |
| Has explicitly asked for lowest possible price | -1 |
| Has compared RCS to a $500 Fiverr option | -2 |

**Minimum required:** 0 or higher. Negative score = disqualify or hold.

---

### Dimension 2: Decision-Making Authority

**Question:** Can this person say yes without approval from someone else?

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Business owner / founder / sole decision-maker | +2 |
| Partner who has budget authority | +1 |
| Manager who needs sign-off but has influence | 0 |
| Contact who must present to committee | -1 |
| “I'll run it by my boss” (unknown authority) | -1 |

**Rule:** If the decision-maker is not on the call, require their presence before sending a proposal. A proposal presented to a non-DM is a proposal that dies.

---

### Dimension 3: Business Stage

**Question:** Is this business at a stage where a premium web investment makes sense?

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Established business (2+ years, active revenue) | +2 |
| Growing business investing in infrastructure | +2 |
| New business with funding or validated concept | +1 |
| New business, no revenue yet, no funding | 0 |
| Pre-idea / “I'm thinking about starting” | -2 |

**Rule:** Pre-revenue businesses with no funding are not RCS clients. They need a DIY solution first. Re-engage when they have revenue and a real problem to solve.

---

### Dimension 4: Urgency

**Question:** Is there a genuine reason to act now, or is this a “nice to have someday”?

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Active pain (losing leads, embarrassed by current site, competitive threat) | +2 |
| Growth milestone (rebrand, expansion, opening, campaign launch) | +2 |
| “We’ve needed to do this for a while” (delayed but real) | +1 |
| “Eventually we’d like to” | -1 |
| No stated urgency or timeline | -1 |

**Rule:** Urgency is not about pressure tactics. It’s about whether the business problem is real and active. A prospect with no urgency is not yet in buying mode.

---

### Dimension 5: Growth Mindset

**Question:** Does this prospect think like a business investor or a cost minimizer?

**Investor mindset signals:**
- “What kind of ROI do your clients typically see?”
- “We’ve lost clients to competitors with better sites — this is costing us”
- “We want to scale and our website doesn’t reflect where we’re going”
- “We’ve already tried the cheap version — it didn’t work”
- Asks about outcomes, not just deliverables

**Cost minimizer signals:**
- “We just need something simple”
- “Can you do it for less?”
- “We had a previous agency and it was a nightmare” (immediately)
- Negotiates before understanding scope
- Asks about ownership, control, and DIY editing before asking about results

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Clear investor mindset — ROI-focused language | +2 |
| Neutral — practical, no strong signals either way | 0 |
| Some cost minimizer signals but open to value framing | -1 |
| Strong cost minimizer — primarily concerned with price | -2 |

## Section 2 — Qualification Score Interpretation

**Maximum score: +10 | Minimum score: -8**

| Score Range | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| +7 to +10 | Ideal client — strong fit across all dimensions | Prioritize. Prepare proposal within 24–48 hours. |
| +4 to +6 | Good client — strong fit, minor gaps | Proceed. Address gaps in proposal or follow-up. |
| +1 to +3 | Possible client — significant qualification work needed | Proceed cautiously. Set expectations explicitly on the call. |
| -1 to 0 | Borderline — one or more disqualifiers present | Use judgment. May require additional qualification conversation. |
| -2 or below | Not an RCS client | Decline gracefully. |

## Section 3 — Disqualification Protocol

### 3.1 How to Decline Gracefully

Declining a prospect is a professional act, not a rejection. Done well, it preserves relationship and reputation.

**If disqualifying on budget:**
```
"I want to be transparent with you, [First Name].

The level of work you're describing — done right — represents an investment
of $[minimum anchor]+. That's not arbitrary; it reflects the strategy,
research, and execution depth that produces the results we're known for.

If that's not where you are right now, I completely understand — and I'd
rather tell you that now than have us both waste time on a scope that
doesn't fit. When the timing is right, I'd love to revisit."
```

**If disqualifying on business stage:**
```
"Based on where [Business Name] is right now, I think a full custom
build might be ahead of where you need to be. The investment won't
pay off until [condition — more volume, more revenue, clearer offer].

I'd rather be honest about that. Let's reconnect in [3–6 months]
when you've hit [milestone]."
```

**If disqualifying on mindset:**
```
"I think we might approach this differently, and I want to be upfront.

Our process is strategy-led and deeply collaborative — it works best
when the client sees the website as a business investment and is
comfortable letting us lead the strategy.

From our conversation, it sounds like you have a very specific vision
and want to direct the creative closely. That's a different kind of
engagement than what we typically take on. I don't want to set
either of us up for friction."
```

### 3.2 The “Not Yet” File

Declined prospects are not deleted. They are placed in a “Not Yet” follow-up file with:
- Reason for declining
- What condition would change the outcome
- Follow-up date (3–6 months)

A business that's pre-revenue today may be a $15,000 client in 12 months. A prospect who couldn't afford RCS's minimums last year may have grown. Maintain the relationship without investing disproportionate time.

## Section 4 — Red Flag Reference

The following behaviors are red flags that warrant heightened caution:

| Red Flag | What It Often Predicts |
|---|---|
| “I've had bad experiences with agencies” (first sentence) | Difficult to satisfy; often the common denominator |
| Negotiates price before seeing scope | Will negotiate every invoice and change order |
| “My nephew does web design but...” | Will second-guess every decision |
| “Just make it look like [competitor]” | No respect for strategic input |
| Vague on what their business does | May not have a viable business model |
| Urgency that's artificially extreme (“Need it in 2 weeks”) | Often a setup for blame |
| Wants to see work “for free” before committing | Low respect for professional work |
| “We'll pay after launch” (refuses deposit) | Financial instability or bad faith |

**Rule:** One red flag does not disqualify. Three red flags in one conversation — walk away.
