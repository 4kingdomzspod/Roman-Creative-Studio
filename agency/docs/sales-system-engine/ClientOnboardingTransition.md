# Client Onboarding Transition
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 5, Document 10

---

### Purpose

The period immediately after a client signs is the most important window in the relationship. The client has made a significant financial commitment and their confidence is at its peak — and also its most fragile. How RCS handles the first 72 hours defines the tone of the entire project.

**Onboarding goal:** Transform a signed contract into a confident client who knows exactly what to expect and trusts the process completely.

---

## Section 1 — The Onboarding Sequence

### Step 1: Payment Confirmation (Same Day as Signing)

Send a payment receipt and confirmation email within 1 hour of deposit received:

```
Subject: Payment Confirmed — Welcome to Roman Creative Studio

Hi [First Name],

Payment confirmed. We're officially underway.

Here's what happens next:

  ✓ I'll send your onboarding questionnaire today
  ✓ You'll have contract + receipt in your inbox within the hour
  ✓ We'll schedule the kickoff call in the next 24–48 hours

I'm looking forward to building this with you.

Alexander
Roman Creative Studio
Alexander@romancreativestudio.co
```

**Rule:** Never let more than 2 hours pass without acknowledging a payment. Silence after payment creates anxiety.

---

### Step 2: Contract Signing (If not already signed)

- Send via PandaDoc / HoneyBook / DocuSign
- Include project agreement with full scope of work, payment schedule, timeline, and revision policy
- Signed copy returned to client immediately upon completion

**Contract must include:**
- [ ] Full scope of work (as written in proposal)
- [ ] "Not Included" list
- [ ] Payment schedule and amounts
- [ ] Timeline with milestone dates
- [ ] Revision policy (e.g., up to 2 rounds of design revisions included)
- [ ] Ownership and IP transfer terms
- [ ] Change order policy (scope changes require new agreement + invoice)
- [ ] Kill fee structure (if project is canceled mid-way)

---

### Step 3: Onboarding Questionnaire (Within 24 Hours of Signing)

Send via Typeform, Notion, or Google Form. This collects everything needed for the kickoff call and strategy phase.

**Questionnaire sections:**

**Business Foundation:**
- What does [Business Name] do? (in your own words, not your website's words)
- Who is your ideal client/patient/customer?
- What problem do you solve for them?
- What makes you different from competitors?

**Goals:**
- What does success look like from this project in 6 months?
- What's the #1 thing you want this website to do for your business?
- Are there specific metrics you'll use to measure success? (lead volume, bookings, calls)

**Brand & Style:**
- 3 words that describe how you want your brand to feel
- 3 competitor or adjacent websites you respect (with brief note on why)
- What do you NOT want your website to feel like?

**Content & Assets:**
- Do you have existing brand guidelines, logo files, or style guides?
- Do you have professional photography? (yes/no/needs to be arranged)
- Do you have existing copy/text for the pages? (yes/no/RCS writing)
- What pages do you know you need?

**Technical:**
- Do you have a domain? Who is it registered with?
- What hosting are you currently on (if any)?
- Are there any third-party integrations needed? (booking, CRM, payments)
- Do you have Google Analytics currently?

**Timeline:**
- Are there any hard launch dates? (event, campaign, season, launch)
- Any planned vacations or periods of unavailability in the next 8 weeks?

---

### Step 4: Kickoff Call Scheduling (Within 48 Hours of Signing)

Schedule a 60-minute kickoff call within the first week.

**Kickoff call agenda:**

```
1. Review questionnaire answers (15 min)
   — Clarify any unclear answers
   — Dig deeper into goals and audience

2. Review scope and timeline (10 min)
   — Confirm pages and features
   — Confirm milestone dates
   — Set communication expectations

3. Content collection plan (15 min)
   — What RCS needs and when
   — Photography: existing or to-source
   — Copy: client-supplied or RCS-written
   — Brand files: logos, existing guidelines

4. Process walkthrough (10 min)
   — How approvals work
   — How revisions work
   — Communication channel (email, Slack, etc.)

5. Questions (10 min)
```

---

After the kickoff call, send a written project timeline:

Excited to build this.

Alexander

## Section 2 — Expectation Setting Standards

### 2.1 Communication Expectations

Set clearly at kickoff:

```
Communication channel: Email (primary) / [Slack / project tool if applicable]
Response time:         Alexander responds to emails within 24 business hours
Client response time:  We ask for 48-hour turnaround on approvals and content requests
Meetings:              Milestone-based, not weekly check-ins (respects both parties' time)
```

### 2.2 Revision Policy

Explain before the design phase begins:

```
Your project includes 2 rounds of design revisions.

A revision round = a consolidated list of changes submitted together.
Not multiple back-and-forth messages over several days.

Here's how to give great feedback:
  ✓ "I'd like the header text larger"
  ✓ "Can we try a darker background on this section?"
  ✘ "I don't know, something just feels off" (not actionable)
  ✘ "My spouse doesn't like it" (not your target customer's opinion)
```

### 2.3 The Golden Rule of Client Management

> "Never surprise a client. Communicate early, communicate proactively, and document everything."

If a timeline is slipping: tell the client 48 hours before the milestone, not the day of.
If scope needs to change: raise it with a change order before doing the work, not after.
If there's a problem: name it directly and bring a solution alongside it.
