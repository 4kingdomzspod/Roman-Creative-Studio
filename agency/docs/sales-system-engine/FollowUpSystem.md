# Follow-Up System
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 5, Document 8

---

### The Follow-Up Principle

The fortune is in the follow-up — but only if the follow-up is worth receiving.

"Just checking in" is not follow-up. It is noise. It creates resentment, not urgency. Every follow-up message must deliver something — a new insight, a relevant example, a useful question, a decision point. The prospect should finish reading the follow-up thinking "that was worth reading" — not "another pushy sales email."

**Rule:** Every follow-up adds value. No exceptions.

---

## Section 1 — Post-Discovery Call Follow-Up

*(When a proposal hasn't been sent yet)*

### Follow-Up 1: 24 Hours After Discovery Call

**Subject:** "Notes from our call + next steps"

```
Hi [First Name],

Great speaking with you today.

Based on our conversation, the three gaps I'd prioritize for
[Business Name] are:

1. [Specific problem #1 from call]
2. [Specific problem #2 from call]
3. [Specific problem #3 from call]

I'll have a proposal ready by [date]. We have our review call
scheduled for [date and time] — I'll walk through everything then.

If anything comes up before that, I'm at Alexander@romancreativestudio.co.

Alexander
```

**Purpose:** Confirms you listened. Maintains momentum. Reminds them of the scheduled review call.

## Section 2 — Post-Proposal Follow-Up

*(After proposal has been sent and reviewed)*

### Follow-Up 1: 24 Hours After Proposal Review Call

**Subject:** "Proposal for [Business Name] — following up"

```
Hi [First Name],

Thanks again for the time yesterday.

The proposal is in your inbox. One thing I'd add after our conversation:
[One specific insight or detail that came up in the review call that
strengthens the case — not a new pitch, a reinforcement].

When you're ready to move forward, here's the link to sign and
submit the deposit: [link]

Any questions before then — just reach out.

Alexander
```

---

### Follow-Up 2: 3 Days After Proposal Review

**Subject:** "One thing for [Business Name] to consider"

This follow-up adds a piece of relevant value — not a nudge to sign:

```
Hi [First Name],

I was looking at [their industry] websites this week and came across
something worth sharing: [specific observation relevant to their situation
— a competitor's website, an industry trend, a case study result].

[One sentence on why it's relevant to their specific situation.]

This is exactly the kind of thing the project we discussed would address.

Let me know if you have questions. Happy to get on a quick call if
anything came up since we last spoke.

Alexander
```

**Value add options:**
- A brief observation about a competitor's website
- A relevant industry statistic (e.g., "82% of dental patients check a practice's website before calling")
- A link to an RCS blog article relevant to their situation
- A before/after from a similar project

---

### Follow-Up 3: 7 Days After Proposal Review

**Subject:** "[Business Name] — checking your timeline"

```
Hi [First Name],

I want to be respectful of both our time, so I'll be direct:
where are you in the decision process for this project?

If the timing has shifted, or if something came up, I'd rather know
than keep following up. If you're still considering it, I'm happy
to answer any remaining questions on a quick call.

Either way — what's the current status?

Alexander
```

**Why this works:** It's honest, direct, and respectful. It gives the prospect permission to say "not now" without ghosting — which is more useful than silence.

---

### Follow-Up 4: 14 Days After Proposal Review (Final)

**Subject:** "Closing the loop on [Business Name] proposal"

```
Hi [First Name],

I want to close the loop on the proposal I sent on [date].

The 14-day investment hold expires this week. I'll re-open
the project timeline when you're ready — but I want to make
sure I'm not holding capacity for a project that's not moving
forward right now.

Two options:

1. You're ready to move — sign here: [link]
2. The timing isn't right — let me know and we'll reconnect
   when circumstances change.

Either is completely fine. I just need to know.

Alexander
```

**Why this works:** Forces a decision. Communicates scarcity (limited capacity) without manipulation. Respects both parties' time. The most professional "final" follow-up possible.

## Section 3 — Long-Term Nurture Follow-Up

For prospects who said "not now" but weren't disqualified — they go into a nurture sequence.

### Nurture Check-In: 60 Days After "Not Now"

```
Hi [First Name],

Checking in from Roman Creative Studio — it's been a couple of months
since we spoke.

We recently completed a project for [similar business type] that saw
[specific result]. I thought of you because the situation was similar
to what you described.

Is [the problem they mentioned] still on your radar? Happy to reconnect
if the timing is better now.

Alexander
```

### Nurture Check-In: 90 Days (Industry Event or Insight)

```
Hi [First Name],

Came across something I thought would be useful for [Business Name]:
[relevant article, statistic, or industry development].

No agenda — just thought it was relevant to what you mentioned
when we last spoke.

Alexander
```

**Nurture rules:**
- Maximum 1 touchpoint per month
- Every touchpoint delivers something useful
- Never pressure. Maintain relationship without needing a response.
- Revisit with a real conversation offer every 60–90 days

## Section 4 — Follow-Up Rules

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| Every follow-up adds value | "Just checking in" trains prospects to ignore you |
| Maximum 4 post-proposal follow-ups | Respect. Beyond 4, they've decided not to. |
| Always reference something specific | Proves you remember them, not a template |
| Never apologize for following up | Signals weakness and unnecessary awkwardness |
| Call instead of email after 3 no-replies | Email is easy to ignore; a call is harder |
| Keep it short | 5–8 sentences maximum |
| One clear CTA per follow-up | Don't give 3 options. Give 1 next step. |
