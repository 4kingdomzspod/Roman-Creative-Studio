## Purpose

Define how Roman Creative Studio expands its reach — geographically, into new client segments, internationally, and through strategic partnerships — in a way that is deliberate, financially sound, and quality-preserving.

**Business Value:** Expansion done wrong destroys companies. Expansion done right creates compounding advantages. This framework ensures every expansion decision is evidence-based, properly resourced, and aligned with our mission — not driven by ego, FOMO, or unsolicited opportunity.

---

## Expansion Principles

1. **Depth before breadth:** Dominate a niche before expanding to adjacent markets
2. **Product before presence:** Geographic expansion follows digital product distribution, not vice versa
3. **Pull before push:** Expand into markets where demand already exists, not to create demand
4. **Profitability preserves quality:** We never expand in a way that forces us to reduce quality
5. **Data-driven decisions:** Every expansion has a hypothesis, success metric, and exit criteria

## Vector 2: Geographic Expansion

### Stage 1: Local Market (Current)
**Definition:** Home city and surrounding metro area
**Strategy:** Build reputation, referral network, and case studies locally
**Success metric:** 3+ clients from referral network; local search ranking for target terms

### Stage 2: Regional Expansion (Year 2–3)
**Definition:** Adjacent cities within 3–4 hours
**Strategy:** Digital-first (no physical presence required); inbound from SEO + content
**Requirements:**
- Proven system for remote client delivery
- Industry landing pages targeting regional cities
- Referral network in each target city (1 contact minimum)
- Case studies relevant to regional market

**Target Regions:** [Define based on founder's networks and current client geography]

### Stage 3: National Presence (Year 3–5)
**Definition:** Serving clients across the US without geographic constraint
**Strategy:** Content + SEO + reputation builds inbound from any location
**Requirements:**
- National brand recognition in 2–3 verticals
- Content strategy targeting national keywords
- Remote delivery fully systematized
- Team capable of covering multiple time zones
- Minimum 2 team members for coverage

**Marketing Investment:** National SEO campaign; targeted content per industry; PR/podcast strategy

### Stage 4: Physical Presence (Year 5+)
**Definition:** Office or co-working presence in second city
**Trigger:** 5+ active clients in a city; or strategic partnership requiring presence
**Requirements:**
- COO or city lead hired for that location
- Legal review: multi-state employment considerations
- Brand guide updated for location-specific marketing
- Minimum 12-month runway of revenue from that market

## Vector 4: Enterprise Client Expansion

**Definition:** Clients with $1M+ revenue seeking $25,000–$150,000+ engagements

### Enterprise Requirements

**Capability Requirements:**
- [ ] Portfolio of 3+ projects at comparable scope and budget
- [ ] Team of minimum 4 people (can't serve enterprise solo)
- [ ] Proven project management process for complex engagements
- [ ] Legal: enterprise contract templates reviewed by CLO
- [ ] Compliance: understand enterprise procurement requirements

**Sales Requirements:**
- RFP (Request for Proposal) response capability
- Enterprise discovery and scoping process
- Reference clients willing to speak to prospective enterprise buyers
- Case studies with measurable business outcomes (not just pretty sites)

**Financial Requirements:**
- Enterprise projects require 25% deposit only (vs. 50% for standard)
- Net-30 payment terms acceptable (vs. milestone-based)
- Insurance: increased liability coverage

**Target Enterprise Profiles:**
- Regional hospital systems (HIPAA-compliant work)
- Franchise systems (national + local presence)
- Law firms with 20+ attorneys
- Regional banks and credit unions

## Vector 5: International Expansion

### Phase 1: English-Speaking Markets (Year 3–5)
**Target Markets:** Canada, United Kingdom, Australia
**Approach:** Digital-first, remote delivery, no physical presence
**Requirements:**
- [ ] Understanding of each market's tax implications for RCS
- [ ] Understanding of local privacy law (PIPEDA in Canada, UK GDPR post-Brexit)
- [ ] Currency and payment infrastructure (Stripe supports multi-currency)
- [ ] Time zone coverage (UK requires early or late hours)
- [ ] Marketing localization (UK English, Canadian market nuances)

**Entry Strategy:** Inbound from content + portfolio; no active outbound to start

### Phase 2: Non-English Markets (Year 7+)
**Approach:** Partnership or acquisition only (not direct entry)
**Requirements:**
- Strategic partner or acquisition target in target country
- Translation and localization capabilities
- Local legal entity if required
- Local leadership

## Vector 6: Strategic Partnerships

### Partnership Types

| Type | Description | Example | Value |
|------|-------------|---------|-------|
| Referral Partner | Send clients to each other | CPA + RCS | Lead flow |
| Technology Partner | Use their tech, they feature us | Vercel, Supabase | Exposure + support |
| Education Partner | Co-create content/curriculum | Design school | Authority |
| Agency Partner | Overflow work, white-label | Non-competing agency | Capacity |
| Distribution Partner | Sell our products | Design marketplace | Revenue |

### Partnership Governance

**Partnership Agreement Requirements:**
- Written agreement for any partnership >$5,000 value exchange
- Clear terms: referral fees, exclusivity, IP ownership, termination
- Annual partnership review: is this delivering value?
- Exit clause: minimum 30-day notice to end

**Partner Evaluation Criteria:**
- [ ] Does the partner serve a similar client profile?
- [ ] Is the partner's quality standard compatible with ours?
- [ ] Is the relationship mutually beneficial (not just one-directional)?
- [ ] Is there a clear commercial agreement?
- [ ] Is there a relationship owner at RCS?

## Expansion Risk Assessment

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Quality dilution in new market | Delivery systems must work in new market before scaling |
| Revenue concentration in new segment | No single client >25% of revenue |
| Legal complexity (multi-state/international) | Legal review before each new jurisdiction |
| Team capacity overrun | Hire 1 step ahead of capacity limits |
| Brand confusion with expansion | Brand architecture documented and enforced |
| Cultural misstep in international markets | Local partner required before direct entry |

## Expansion Decision Framework

Before committing to any expansion vector, answer:

1. **Is there demonstrated demand?** (Not assumed — evidenced by inbound inquiries, referrals, or market data)
2. **Do we have the capacity?** (People, process, and budget to execute without harming existing business)
3. **Is the margin acceptable?** (Expansion must maintain ≥60% gross margin)
4. **What's the exit?** (If this expansion fails after 12 months, what do we do?)
5. **Who owns it?** (Named person accountable for this expansion's success)
