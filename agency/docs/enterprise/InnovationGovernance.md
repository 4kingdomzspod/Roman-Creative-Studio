## Purpose

Define the governance structure that ensures RCS’s innovation activities are strategically aligned, properly resourced, adequately controlled, and never allowed to distract from or damage the core agency business.

**Business Value:** Innovation without governance is gambling. Governance without innovation is stagnation. This framework creates the conditions for ambitious experimentation within responsible boundaries, ensuring that bold bets are made with clear criteria, defined budgets, and honest evaluation.

---

## Innovation Governance Principles

1. **Agency first:** The innovation program never compromises client delivery or agency profitability.
2. **Evidence over enthusiasm:** We build on validated signals, not excitement about an idea.
3. **Budget discipline:** All innovation spending is approved and tracked. No unplanned innovation spend.
4. **Portfolio thinking:** We manage innovation as a portfolio — some bets will fail; that’s expected and planned for.
5. **Time-boxing:** Every experiment has an end date. Zombie projects are terminated.
6. **Transparency:** Innovation status is reported honestly, including failures.

## Innovation Governance Bodies

### Innovation Committee
**Stage:** Active from Stage 2
**Members:** CEO + CIO + CPO
**Cadence:** Monthly (60 minutes)
**Authority:**
- Approve new experiments (up to $1,000 budget)
- Approve new product builds (up to $10,000)
- Kill experiments that are not meeting criteria
- Adjust innovation time allocation
- Report innovation status to Leadership Committee

**Agenda Template:**
1. Pipeline status: all experiments and builds, RAG status (15 min)
2. New ideas: score any Stage 0 items (15 min)
3. Kill/proceed decisions on any flagged items (15 min)
4. Budget review: spend vs. allocation (10 min)
5. Upcoming: what’s launching next quarter (5 min)

---

### CEO Innovation Review
**Cadence:** Quarterly (30 minutes)
**Purpose:** CEO checks in on the full innovation portfolio
**Questions answered:**
- Is innovation consuming the right % of resources?
- Are we making progress on our highest-priority bets?
- Are we killing things that aren’t working fast enough?
- What does the innovation portfolio look like financially?
- Any innovation activities creating risk for the agency?

## Innovation Budget Governance

### Annual Budget Allocation
| Year | Innovation Budget | % of Revenue | Approval Authority |
|------|-----------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Year 1 | $2,000 | ~3% | CEO |
| Year 2 | $8,000 | ~3% | CEO + CFO |
| Year 3 | $20,000 | ~3% | CEO + CFO |
| Year 4+ | ~3% of revenue | Ongoing | CFO |

### Spend Tiers

| Amount | Type | Approval Required |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| <$100 | Micro-experiment (surveys, landing pages) | CIO self-approve |
| $100–$500 | Standard experiment | Innovation Committee |
| $500–$5,000 | Prototype build | CEO + CIO |
| $5,000–$25,000 | Full product build | CEO + CFO |
| >$25,000 | Major investment | CEO + CFO + Board (Stage 4+) |

### Budget Tracking
- All innovation spend tracked in dedicated budget line in financial dashboard
- Monthly variance report: budget vs. actual
- Any overrun >10% requires CEO approval before continuing
- Unspent budget does NOT roll over — resets annually (prevents accumulation without accountability)

## Innovation Time Governance

### Time Allocation by Stage

| Stage | CEO Innovation Time | Developer Time | Designer Time |
|-------|--------------------|--------------|------------------|
| Stage 1 (solo) | 10% (4 hrs/wk) | 0% dedicated | 0% dedicated |
| Stage 2 (2–4 people) | 15% (6 hrs/wk) | 10% | 10% |
| Stage 3 (5–10 people) | 20% (8 hrs/wk) | 20% | 15% |
| Stage 4 (10–25 people) | 25% (10 hrs/wk) | 30% dedicated | 20% dedicated |

### Time Protection Rules
- Innovation time is scheduled in advance (blocked on calendar)
- Innovation time is cancelled only for genuine client emergencies
- Cancelled innovation time is rescheduled within the same week
- Innovation time is tracked separately from billable hours

## Innovation Portfolio Management

### Portfolio Composition Target (Mature Stage)

| Category | % of Portfolio | Horizon | Risk Level |
|----------|--------------|---------|------------|
| Near-term (enhance existing) | 70% | 0–12 months | Low |
| Mid-term (new products) | 20% | 1–3 years | Medium |
| Long-term (transformational) | 10% | 3+ years | High |

**Explanation:** Most innovation time should improve what already exists. A smaller portion explores new products. The smallest slice makes big bets.

## Innovation Decision Framework

Every new innovation idea is scored on 6 dimensions (1–3 each, maximum 18):

| Dimension | 1 (Low) | 2 (Medium) | 3 (High) |
|-----------|---------|------------|----------|
| Market demand | Unclear | Indicated | Validated |
| Strategic alignment | Tangential | Related | Core |
| RCS capability | New skill required | Adjacent | We already do this |
| Revenue potential | <$500/mo | $500–2k/mo | >$2k/mo |
| Time to revenue | >18 months | 6–18 months | <6 months |
| Risk level | High | Medium | Low |

**Threshold:** Score ≥15 to advance. Score 10–14: watch list. Score <10: deprioritize.

## Innovation Ethics

### Standards for RCS Innovation

**AI Products:**
- All AI outputs that reach clients must be reviewed by a human
- No AI product makes consequential decisions without human oversight
- We do not build products that deceive users into thinking they’re talking to a human
- AI training data sourced ethically; no use of data without appropriate rights

**Data Products:**
- We do not sell user data to third parties
- We do not build surveillance or behavioral manipulation tools
- User privacy is protected in every product we launch

**Competitive Ethics:**
- We do not build products that replicate competitors’ IP
- We do not use client relationship access to gather competitive intelligence
- We credit and compensate inspiration sources appropriately

## Kill Criteria (Non-Negotiable)

Any initiative is immediately terminated if:
- It requires compromising client delivery
- It requires exceeding the approved budget without new approval
- Evidence clearly shows the market does not want the product (after fair validation)
- The team responsible has lost conviction and is continuing out of sunk-cost bias
- It creates legal, security, or reputational risk to RCS

**Kill process:** CIO recommends kill → CEO approves → documented in Innovation Pipeline → learnings captured

## Intellectual Property Governance

### IP Ownership
- All innovation work done on RCS time is owned by RCS
- Contractors contributing to innovation work must have IP assignment in their contract
- Open-source contributions require CEO approval before releasing
- Third-party IP incorporated in products must have appropriate license

### IP Protection
- Novel product concepts: document with dated internal memo before public announcement
- Software: copyright notice on all original code
- Brand elements (logos, names): trademark registration when product reaches $50k ARR
- Domain names for future products: register early (low cost, high protection)
