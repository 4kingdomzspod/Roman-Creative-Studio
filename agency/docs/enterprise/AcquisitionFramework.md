## Purpose

Establish a disciplined framework for evaluating, executing, and integrating potential acquisitions — both acquiring other companies to accelerate growth AND understanding what makes RCS an attractive acquisition target if that path is ever pursued.

**Business Value:** Acquisitions offer the fastest path to new capabilities, client bases, and market positions. They also carry the highest risk of any growth strategy. This framework ensures we never make an emotionally-driven acquisition and always have a clear integration plan before signing.

**Note:** Acquisitions are unlikely before Year 4–5. This document is built now so the thinking is done before the opportunity appears.

---

## Acquisition Philosophy

1. **Buy talent and clients, not just revenue:** The best acquisitions add team capability and client relationships, not just a revenue number.
2. **Culture first:** No acquisition makes sense if the culture is incompatible. Culture integration is the hardest part of M&A.
3. **Conservative valuation:** We pay for what we can verify, not what we're promised.
4. **Integration plan before closing:** If we don't know exactly how we'll integrate this, we don't close.
5. **Small before large:** First acquisition should be a small agency or product (<$500k deal size) to develop our integration muscle.

## Acquisition Types

### Type A: Talent Acquisition (Acqui-hire)
**What it is:** Acquiring a small agency primarily to bring on their team
**Typical size:** 1–5 person agency; deal <$200k
**What we gain:** Skills, relationships, capacity
**Integration complexity:** Low (team joins RCS; client base evaluated case by case)

### Type B: Client Base Acquisition
**What it is:** Acquiring an agency to take on their recurring client relationships
**Typical size:** Agency with $5k–20k MRR; deal $50k–$300k
**What we gain:** Immediate MRR, existing relationships
**Integration complexity:** Medium (clients must be transitioned; they may leave)

### Type C: Product Acquisition
**What it is:** Acquiring a digital product (template library, SaaS tool, course) to add to our product portfolio
**Typical size:** Product with $1k–10k MRR; deal $50k–$500k
**What we gain:** Immediate product revenue, validated product-market fit
**Integration complexity:** Low-Medium (technology integration needed)

### Type D: Strategic Acquisition
**What it is:** Acquiring a company that gives us a capability or market position we couldn't build fast enough
**Typical size:** Company with $500k–2M ARR; deal $1M–5M
**What we gain:** Market position, technology, team, clients
**Integration complexity:** High (requires dedicated integration team)

## Acquisition Evaluation Criteria

### Phase 1: Screening (Before Deeper Diligence)

Answer all of these before spending serious time:

| Question | Red Flag Answer |
|----------|----------------|
| Does this align with our 3-year strategic plan? | No |
| Is the asking price within our financial capacity? | >2× our annual profit |
| Is the culture compatible with RCS values? | Major conflicts |
| Is the key person planning to leave immediately? | Yes (talent acquisition only) |
| Is there a clear, provable reason the owner is selling? | Unclear or suspicious |
| Is the revenue independently verifiable? | No |

**Minimum financial threshold:** We only pursue acquisitions when we have 12 months of operating runway AFTER the acquisition cost.

### Phase 2: Strategic Fit Assessment

**Scoring Matrix (1–5 each):**

| Dimension | Weight | Score | Weighted Score |
|-----------|--------|-------|----------------|
| Strategic alignment (supports 3-year plan) | 25% | | |
| Team quality and cultural fit | 20% | | |
| Client quality and retention likelihood | 20% | | |
| Revenue quality (recurring vs. one-time) | 15% | | |
| Technology and IP value | 10% | | |
| Integration complexity (5 = easy) | 10% | | |
| **Total** | **100%** | | |

**Threshold:** Weighted score ≥3.5 to proceed to due diligence.

### Phase 3: Due Diligence

#### Financial Due Diligence
- [ ] 3 years of financial statements (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow)
- [ ] MRR/ARR verified against bank statements
- [ ] Client contract list: term, renewal date, monthly value
- [ ] All expenses verified (no hidden costs)
- [ ] Outstanding liabilities identified (debt, leases, legal)
- [ ] Tax returns for past 3 years
- [ ] Any pending or threatened litigation
- [ ] Owner compensation normalized (add back to get true EBITDA)

#### Legal Due Diligence
- [ ] Corporate structure and ownership verified
- [ ] All client contracts reviewed (assignment clauses, change of control)
- [ ] Employee/contractor agreements reviewed
- [ ] IP ownership verified (they own what they say they own)
- [ ] No IP infringement claims active or threatened
- [ ] All licenses, permits in good standing
- [ ] No regulatory violations or investigations

#### Operational Due Diligence
- [ ] Key processes documented (or undocumented — document risk)
- [ ] Technology stack assessed (what are we buying?)
- [ ] Client relationships assessed (key contacts willing to stay?)
- [ ] Team assessment (who is essential? who might leave?)
- [ ] Systems integration complexity assessed
- [ ] Culture assessment completed (team interviews)

#### Client Due Diligence
- [ ] Top 10 clients by revenue identified
- [ ] Client health score assessed
- [ ] Churn risk assessed (any clients likely to leave with seller?)
- [ ] NPS or satisfaction data available?
- [ ] Client references contacted (with seller's permission)

### Phase 4: Valuation

**Common Valuation Methods for Small Agencies:**

| Method | Formula | When to Use |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| Revenue Multiple | 0.5–1.5× Annual Revenue | For agencies with unreliable profit |
| EBITDA Multiple | 3–5× EBITDA | For profitable agencies |
| MRR Multiple | 24–48× Monthly MRR | For recurring revenue focused |
| Asset Value | Sum of tangible and intangible assets | For product acquisitions |

**RCS Valuation Principles:**
- Never pay more than 3× verified annual EBITDA for a service business
- Discount for: key person risk, client concentration, undocumented processes
- Premium for: strong MRR, documented systems, experienced team willing to stay
- Always negotiate on earnout structure (portion of payment tied to performance post-acquisition)

---

### Phase 5: Deal Structure

**Preferred Deal Structures:**

| Structure | How It Works | Risk Profile |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **All cash at close** | Full payment at signing | High risk for buyer; simple |
| **Cash + earnout** | Portion paid over 12–24 months based on performance | Preferred — aligns incentives |
| **Equity swap** | Seller receives RCS equity instead of cash | Complex; requires valuation |
| **Asset purchase** | Buy assets, not the entity | Cleaner; avoids unknown liabilities |
| **Stock purchase** | Buy the entity | Takes on all liabilities |

**RCS Preferred Structure:** Asset purchase with cash + earnout
- 50–60% cash at close
- 40–50% earnout over 18–24 months tied to retained MRR and client satisfaction

## Integration Plan

### Pre-Close (30 days before)
- [ ] Integration team named
- [ ] Day 1 communication plan for clients prepared
- [ ] Day 1 communication plan for acquired team prepared
- [ ] Technology integration plan documented
- [ ] Client contract assignment strategy decided
- [ ] Brand transition plan decided

### Day 1
- [ ] Announce to clients: warm, personal communication from RCS leadership + seller
- [ ] Announce to team: welcome, roles, compensation confirmation
- [ ] IT access: new accounts created; old credentials collected
- [ ] Payment systems: billing transferred to RCS
- [ ] Contracts: counterparties notified of assignment

### Month 1
- [ ] All clients met (video call minimum)
- [ ] All team members on RCS systems
- [ ] Processes documented
- [ ] Any quality issues identified and addressed
- [ ] Financial integration complete

### Month 2–6: Deep Integration
- [ ] Brand transition completed (if changing to RCS brand)
- [ ] Processes fully aligned to RCS standards
- [ ] Team fully onboarded (culture, tools, performance framework)
- [ ] Client retention rate monitored monthly (target: >90%)
- [ ] Earnout milestone tracking active

### Month 12: Integration Review
- [ ] Client retention final assessment
- [ ] Team retention and performance assessment
- [ ] Financial performance vs. acquisition thesis
- [ ] Lessons learned documented (improves next acquisition)

## RCS as an Acquisition Target

Understanding what makes RCS attractive to potential acquirers helps us build a more valuable company.

### What Acquirers Value

| Attribute | How We Build It |
|-----------|----------------|
| Recurring revenue (MRR/ARR) | Care Plans, community, SaaS |
| Documented systems | This operating system |
| Diversified revenue streams | Agency + products + education |
| Strong client NPS | Quality management system |
| Trained, retained team | HR OS + culture |
| Intellectual property | Templates, courses, SaaS, brand |
| National reach | Content + SEO + reputation |
| Low founder dependency | Succession planning |

### Valuation Impact of This Operating System
A company with documented systems is worth 30–50% more than an equivalent company where knowledge lives only in the founder's head. Every document in this repository is an asset that increases enterprise value.

### Acquisition Exit Considerations (Year 8–15)
- **Strategic buyer:** Larger agency or holding company acquires RCS for client base + team + brand
- **Private equity:** PE firm buys majority stake to accelerate growth (founder retains minority)
- **Management buyout:** Leadership team buys out founder over 3–5 years
- **Merger:** RCS merges with compatible agency of similar size for scale
