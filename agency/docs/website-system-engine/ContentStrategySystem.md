# Content Strategy System
## Roman Creative Studio — Phase 4, Document 11

---

### Content's Role in the AOS

Content serves three simultaneous functions for Roman Creative Studio:

1. **SEO** — attracts organic traffic through relevant, keyword-targeted pages and articles
2. **Trust** — demonstrates authority and expertise before a visitor ever contacts the agency
3. **Conversion** — moves visitors through the decision process by answering the right questions in the right order

Content that doesn't serve at least one of these functions doesn't belong on the website.

## Section 1 — Content Hierarchy

### 1.1 Content Types

| Type | Volume | Primary Goal | Secondary Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core pages (Home, About, Services, etc.) | 7 pages | Convert | Build trust |
| Service pages | 6 pages | Convert + SEO | Demonstrate expertise |
| Industry pages | 7 pages | SEO + Convert | Demonstrate vertical depth |
| Case studies | Growing | Convert | Build trust |
| Blog articles | Growing | SEO | Build authority |
| FAQ content | 1 page | Convert (objection handling) | SEO (question keywords) |

### 1.2 Content Priority Order

Create content in this order:

```
Phase 1 (Foundation):   Core pages + service pages + industry pages
Phase 2 (Proof):        3–5 case studies
Phase 3 (Authority):    4 pillar blog articles
Phase 4 (Growth):       Blog expansion + long-tail content
```

Do not start blogging before foundational pages are complete. A blog with no home base has no conversion destination.

## Section 2 — Blog Strategy

### 2.1 Blog Purpose

The RCS blog exists to:
1. Rank for informational keywords that funnel into service/industry pages
2. Demonstrate subject-matter expertise to prospective clients
3. Build topical authority that lifts all pages in the same silo

The blog does NOT exist to:
- Share company news (no one outside RCS cares)
- Post team birthday announcements
- Publish vague motivational content
- Compete with service page keywords

### 2.2 Blog Content Categories

**Category 1: Website Strategy** (drives to service pages)
```
Examples:
"How Much Does a Custom Website Cost in 2024?"
"Custom Website vs. Website Builder: What Service Businesses Need to Know"
"7 Signs Your Website Is Actively Losing You Clients"
"Why Your Website's Homepage Is Failing (And How to Fix It)"
"What Is Conversion Rate Optimization and Why Does It Matter?"
```

**Category 2: Industry Guides** (drives to industry pages)
```
Examples:
"The Complete Dental Practice Website Checklist"
"How to Choose a Web Designer for Your Church"
"Restaurant Website Must-Haves: What Diners Look For Before They Visit"
"What Makes a Great Healthcare Website in 2024?"
"Real Estate Website Design: How Agents Win More Leads Online"
```

**Category 3: SEO & Marketing** (attracts business owners interested in growth)
```
Examples:
"Local SEO for Service Businesses: A No-Nonsense Guide"
"How to Get Your Business on Page 1 of Google"
"What Are Core Web Vitals and Why Should You Care?"
"Why Your Google Business Profile Is Your Most Underused Asset"
```

**Category 4: Case Study Articles** (proof content that ranks for branded + project searches)
```
Examples:
"How We Helped [Dental Practice] Increase New Patient Inquiries by 30%"
"A Church Website Redesign: What We Changed and Why It Worked"
```

### 2.3 Article Structure Template

Every blog article follows this structure:

```
1. H1 Title (primary keyword target)
2. Introduction (100–150 words)
   - Hook: the problem or question
   - Why this matters to the reader
   - What they'll learn (don't tease — tell them)
3. Article body (800–2000 words typical)
   - H2 sections for each major point
   - H3 subsections where needed
   - Short paragraphs (3–4 sentences max)
   - Bullet points for scannable lists
   - One callout/highlight box per article (key insight)
4. Mid-article CTA (contextual, non-intrusive)
   - Relevant to the article topic
   - Links to a service or industry page
   - Not "Buy now" — "Learn how we approach this"
5. Conclusion (100–150 words)
   - Summarize key takeaways
   - Reinforce the value of the insight
   - Soft CTA to contact or book a call
6. Related articles (2–3 links to related content)
```

### 2.4 Blog SEO Rules

- **H1:** Contains primary keyword naturally
- **Length:** Minimum 800 words; 1500–2000 for competitive keywords
- **Internal links:** 2–3 links to relevant service or industry pages per article
- **CTA:** At least 1 mid-article and 1 end-of-article contextual CTA
- **Images:** At least 1 relevant image with descriptive alt text
- **Schema:** `Article` JSON-LD on every blog post
- **URL:** `/blog/[keyword-rich-slug]/`
- **Publish frequency:** Quality over frequency. 1 high-quality article beats 4 shallow ones.

### 2.5 Blog Publishing Cadence (Recommended)

| Period | Target |
|---|---|
| Month 1–2 | 4 foundational pillar articles (one per category) |
| Month 3–6 | 1–2 articles/month |
| Month 6+ | 2–4 articles/month as capacity allows |

## Section 3 — Case Study Content Structure

### 3.1 Case Study Brief Template

For every completed project, capture:

```
CLIENT BRIEF:
- Client name and business type
- Industry vertical
- Location
- Business size (rough — solo, small team, multi-location)

PROJECT BRIEF:
- What service(s) were delivered
- Timeline
- Key challenges going in

RESULTS:
- Primary metric (quantified if possible)
- Secondary metrics
- Client quote (written permission required)

VISUALS:
- Before screenshot (if redesign)
- After screenshot / mockup
- Any in-process work worth showing
```

### 3.2 Case Study Writing Rules

1. **Lead with the result, not the process.** Readers want to know the outcome first.
2. **Use the client's language** for describing their problem — not agency jargon.
3. **Quantify everything possible.** "Increased traffic" is weak. "+67% organic traffic in 3 months" is proof.
4. **If no metrics exist:** a strong, specific testimonial is acceptable. Never invent numbers.
5. **Tell a story.** Challenge → Approach → Result is a narrative arc, not a feature list.

## Section 4 — Conversion Copywriting Principles

### 4.1 The Visitor-First Rule

Every sentence on every page must serve the visitor, not the agency.

| Agency-First (Wrong) | Visitor-First (Right) |
|---|---|
| "We are a premium web design studio" | "Your website should be as good as your business" |
| "We have 5 years of experience" | "We've built websites for businesses just like yours" |
| "Our process is proven" | "You'll know exactly what's happening at every step" |
| "We offer a range of services" | "Whether you need a brand, a website, or both — we can help" |

### 4.2 Outcome Language

Describe what the visitor gets, not what RCS does.

| Feature Language (Wrong) | Outcome Language (Right) |
|---|---|
| "Custom design" | "A website that looks like no one else's" |
| "Mobile-responsive" | "Looks perfect on every phone your clients use" |
| "SEO optimization" | "Clients in your city find you before they find your competition" |
| "Fast load times" | "Visitors don't leave before the page loads" |
| "Professional photography direction" | "Photos that make your work look as good as it actually is" |

### 4.3 Prohibited Language (from Phase 2C ContentMessagingGovernance)

Never use:
- "cutting-edge" / "innovative" / "disruptive"
- "leverage" / "synergy" / "seamless"
- "world-class" / "best-in-class"
- "passionate about" (every agency says this)
- "one-stop shop"
- "we take pride in"
- "our team of experts"

### 4.4 Reading Level Target

- Target Grade 8–10 reading level (Flesch-Kincaid)
- Short sentences: average 15–18 words
- Short paragraphs: 2–4 sentences per paragraph
- Active voice > passive voice
- Contractions are fine ("you'll" not "you will" — less formal, more human)

### 4.5 Headlines: The 4 Us Formula

Every headline should satisfy at least 2 of the 4 Us:
1. **Useful** — provides value or solves a problem
2. **Urgent** — creates a reason to act now (without being fake)
3. **Unique** — says something competitors don't
4. **Ultra-specific** — uses numbers, names, timeframes

```
"7 Signs Your Website Is Losing You Clients" → Useful + Ultra-specific (number)
"Dental Websites That Book More Patients" → Useful + Unique (industry-specific)
"Free 30-Minute Discovery Call" → Urgent + Ultra-specific (timeframe)
```

## Section 5 — Content Governance

### 5.1 Content Review Schedule

| Content Type | Review Frequency | Check For |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Quarterly | Metrics accuracy, testimonial freshness, CTA relevance |
| Service pages | Bi-annually | Pricing references, process accuracy, case study links |
| Industry pages | Bi-annually | Industry-specific accuracy, example relevance |
| Blog articles | Annually | Statistics currency, link validity, keyword ranking |
| Case studies | As needed | Client permission status, metrics accuracy |

### 5.2 Content Quality Gates

Before publishing any page or article:
- [ ] H1 contains primary keyword
- [ ] Meta title and description are written (unique, within length limits)
- [ ] At least 2 internal links to relevant pages
- [ ] At least 1 CTA to a conversion page
- [ ] All claims are accurate and verifiable
- [ ] No prohibited language from Phase 2C governance
- [ ] Images have descriptive alt text
- [ ] Reading level is Grade 8–10
- [ ] Proofread: no spelling errors, no grammar issues
